# Critical and Effective Thinking: Resources

All entries were opened and checked on 2026-10-16. Quotes used in lessons were checked against the loaded text. "Caveat" lines say what was not checked or how strong the evidence is.

## Knowledge

### The whole chain (core frameworks)

- [Guide: _The Miniature Guide to Critical Thinking: Concepts and Tools_ by Richard Paul & Linda Elder (Foundation for Critical Thinking)](https://www.criticalthinking.org/store/products/critical-thinking-concepts-amp-tools-8th-edition/156) ([free 4th-edition excerpt, 2006](https://www.criticalthinking.org/files/Concepts_Tools.pdf))
  Elements of thought (p. 5), nine intellectual standards (p. 12), intellectual traits. Use for: mapping elements onto the four links; checklist items for each standard. Caveat: the Foundation's [standards web page](https://www.criticalthinking.org/pages/universal-intellectual-standards/527) lists eight (no "significance"); the guide lists nine. Cite one version consistently.
- [Web pages: Foundation for Critical Thinking: elements and standards](https://www.criticalthinking.org/pages/the-elements-of-reasoning-and-the-intellectual-standards/480), [Valuable Intellectual Traits](https://www.criticalthinking.org/pages/valuable-intellectual-traits/528)
  Exact guiding questions for each standard ("Is that really true? How could we check that?") and definitions of each trait. Use for: wording of check questions.
- [Report: _Critical Thinking: A Statement of Expert Consensus_ (the APA "Delphi Report") by Peter A. Facione, 1990](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED315423) ([full scan](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED315423.pdf); [2023 essay "Critical Thinking: What It Is and Why It Counts"](https://insightassessment.com/iaresource/critical-thinking-what-it-is-and-why-it-counts/))
  Expert-consensus definition, six core skills, and dispositions of "the ideal critical thinker" (p. 3, p. 8). Use for: the "skills vs. dispositions" layer; self-regulation.
- [Article: "Validity and Soundness", _Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy_](https://iep.utm.edu/val-snd/)
  "A deductive argument is sound if and only if it is both valid, and all of its premises are actually true." Use for: why Ground and Reasoning are separate links.

### Question link

- [Book: _How to Solve It_ by George Pólya (Princeton University Press, 1945)](https://press.princeton.edu/books/paperback/9780691164076/how-to-solve-it) ([scan of the question list, pp. xvi–xvii, Harvard course page](https://legacy-www.math.harvard.edu/~knill/teaching/math22a2018/lit/PolyaHowToSolveIt.pdf))
  Four phases and the classic question list: "What is the unknown? What are the data? What is the condition?" Use for: understanding the problem before working on it; "looking back".
- [Talk: "You and Your Research" by Richard Hamming (1986), transcript](https://www.cs.virginia.edu/~robins/YouAndYourResearch.html)
  "What are the important problems of your field?"; "If you do not work on an important problem, it's unlikely you'll do important work."; a problem is important only if "you have a reasonable attack"; "Great Thoughts Time" at Friday lunch. Use for: choosing which question deserves your brainpower.
- [Book: 安宅和人『イシューからはじめよ――知的生産の「シンプルな本質」』(英治出版, 2010; 改訂版 2024)](https://eijipress.co.jp/products/2356) ([author's 2008 blog post that the book grew from](https://kaz-ataka.hatenablog.com/entry/20081018/1224287687))
  Issue-driven work: find the issue first; the "dog's path" (犬の道) of doing everything by effort; issue degree × quality of answer. Use for: Lesson 2 (question as a filter). Caveat: no official English translation ("Issue Driven" is only the parallel title). The three conditions of a good issue (本質的な選択肢である・深い仮説がある・答えを出せる) and "2–3 problems out of 100" were checked only in secondary summaries.
- [Book: _Bulletproof Problem Solving_ by Charles Conn & Robert McLean (Wiley, 2018)](https://www.wiley.com/en-us/Bulletproof+Problem+Solving%3A+The+One+Skill+That+Changes+Everything-p-9781119553021) ([Chapter 1 excerpt](https://media.wiley.com/product_data/excerpt/24/11195530/1119553024-191.pdf))
  Seven steps, starting with "Define the problem". A good problem definition is "specific, not general", has measurable success, is bounded in time, and "involves definitive action being taken". Use for: rewriting topics into decision-ready questions.
- [Method: Question Formulation Technique, Right Question Institute](https://rightquestion.org/what-is-the-qft/)
  A protocol for producing, improving and prioritizing questions ("Ask as many questions as you can."). Use for: a practice drill when the user has a topic but no question.

### Ground link

- [Text: Aristotle, _Physics_ I.1 (trans. Hardie & Gaye, MIT Classics)](http://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/physics.1.i.html), [_Metaphysics_ Δ.1 (trans. Ross)](http://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/metaphysics.5.v.html), [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: "Aristotle"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/aristotle/)
  What "first principles" means: knowing a thing means carrying "our analysis as far as its simplest elements"; a first principle is "the first point from which a thing either is or comes to be or is known". Use for: Lesson 3.
- [Talk: Elon Musk with Chris Anderson, TED2013 (transcript)](https://www.ted.com/talks/elon_musk_the_mind_behind_tesla_spacex_solarcity)
  "boil things down to their fundamental truths and reason up from there, as opposed to reasoning by analogy." Use for: first principles vs. analogy. Caveat: the battery-cost example comes from a 2012 interview that exists only in secondary transcripts that disagree; paraphrase it, don't quote it.
- [Chapter: "The Fine Art of Baloney Detection" by Carl Sagan, _The Demon-Haunted World_ (1995), as reproduced by The Marginalian](https://www.themarginalian.org/2014/01/03/baloney-detection-kit-carl-sagan/)
  Nine tools: independent confirmation, more than one hypothesis, quantify, "every link in the chain must work (including the premise)". Use for: checking claims from unknown sources. Caveat: wording checked only against this reproduction.

### Reasoning link

- [Paper: "The Method of Multiple Working Hypotheses" by T. C. Chamberlin (_Science_, 1890), scan of the original](https://archive.org/details/jstor-1764336)
  How a favorite explanation hardens into "a ruling theory"; the fix is to keep several hypotheses alive. Use for: Lesson 5 (rival explanations).
- [Book: _The Scout Mindset_ by Julia Galef (Portfolio, 2021)](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/555240/the-scout-mindset-by-julia-galef/) ([her definitions on the Mindscape podcast](https://www.preposterousuniverse.com/podcast/2021/04/19/143-julia-galef-on-openness-bias-and-rationality/))
  Soldier vs. scout mindset; five self-tests (double standard, outsider, conformity, selective skeptic, status quo). Use for: checking your own motivated reasoning; the "traits" layer as behaviors. Caveat: the test wording was checked only in a [chapter outline](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/HDAXztEbjJsyHLKP7/outline-of-galef-s-scout-mindset).
- [Article: "Before You Make That Big Decision..." by Daniel Kahneman, Dan Lovallo & Olivier Sibony (_HBR_, June 2011)](https://hbr.org/2011/06/the-big-idea-before-you-make-that-big-decision)
  A 12-question checklist for reviewing a recommendation (e.g., "Are credible alternatives included along with the recommendation?"); reviewers must be independent "because you can't recognize your own biases". Use for: AI-as-reviewer prompts. Caveat: a practitioner tool, not validated.
- [Article: "Performing a Project Premortem" by Gary Klein (_HBR_, September 2007)](https://hbr.org/2007/09/performing-a-project-premortem)
  Assume the project "has just failed", then each person independently lists reasons. Use for: a rally role ("assume my conclusion turned out wrong; why?").

### So-what link

- [Book: _The Pyramid Principle_ by Barbara Minto (author's site)](https://www.barbaraminto.com/) (Japanese edition: 『考える技術・書く技術』ダイヤモンド社)
  Present ideas "organized as a pyramid under a single point"; the Situation–Complication–Question framework finds the reader's question. Use for: leading with the so-what.
- [Book: _Superforecasting_ by Philip Tetlock & Dan Gardner (Crown, 2015)](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/227815/superforecasting-by-philip-e-tetlock-and-dan-gardner/) ([the "Ten Commandments" appendix, reproduced on LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/dvYeSKDRd68GcrWoe/ten-commandments-for-aspiring-superforecasters))
  "Triage" ("Focus on questions where your hard work is likely to pay off"), degrees of doubt, updating on evidence. Use for: "what would change my mind", confidence levels, where to spend effort.

### Not fooling yourself, checklists, and AI

- [Talk: "Cargo Cult Science" by Richard Feynman (Caltech, 1974)](https://calteches.library.caltech.edu/51/2/CargoCult.htm)
  "The first principle is that you must not fool yourself—and you are the easiest person to fool."; "bending over backwards to show how you're maybe wrong". Use for: why a checklist and a critical partner are needed. Primary source of Lesson 1.
- [Book: _The Checklist Manifesto_ by Atul Gawande (Metropolitan Books, 2009)](https://psnet.ahrq.gov/issue/checklist-manifesto-how-get-things-right) ([his New Yorker article, 2007](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2007/12/10/the-checklist))
  DO-CONFIRM vs. READ-DO; "between five and nine items"; "simple and exact" wording; one page; test in the real world. Use for: Lesson 7 (designing the checklist). Caveat: book quotes were checked via a Kindle-highlight export, so page numbers are not verified.
- [Paper: "The Impact of Generative AI on Critical Thinking" by Hao-Ping Lee et al. (CHI 2025, Microsoft Research)](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/the-impact-of-generative-ai-on-critical-thinking-self-reported-reductions-in-cognitive-effort-and-confidence-effects-from-a-survey-of-knowledge-workers/)
  319 knowledge workers, 936 examples: "higher confidence in GenAI is associated with less critical thinking, while higher self-confidence is associated with more critical thinking." Use for: "think first, then ask". Caveat: self-reported and cross-sectional (a link, not a cause).
- [Paper: "Towards Understanding Sycophancy in Language Models" by Mrinank Sharma et al. (ICLR 2024, Anthropic)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.13548)
  Five AI assistants "consistently exhibit sycophancy"; humans sometimes prefer convincing sycophantic answers over correct ones. Use for: designing rally prompts that resist agreement. Caveat: 2023 models.
- [Tools: Clearer Thinking (Spencer Greenberg)](https://www.clearerthinking.org/)
  "Over 80" free tools for decisions and thinking. Use for: optional solo practice between lessons.

## Wisdom (Communities)

The user's preference about joining communities has not been asked yet.

- [カフェフィロ (Cafe Philo)](https://cafephilo.jp/)
  Philosophy-dialogue group founded in 2005 by people from Osaka University's clinical philosophy lab; online and in-person dialogues, Socratic Dialogue sessions. Use for: practicing questioning and listening with real people, in Japanese.
- [Good Judgment Open](https://www.gjopen.com/)
  Public forecasting questions from Good Judgment (Tetlock's group), scored for accuracy. Use for: real feedback on how well you reason under uncertainty and update your mind. Questions lean toward geopolitics and economics.
- [Metaculus](https://www.metaculus.com/questions/)
  Forecasting platform with scored track records and tournaments; topics lean toward AI and science. Use for: the same as Good Judgment Open, closer to the user's field.
- [日本ディベート協会 (Japan Debate Association)](https://japan-debate-association.org/)
  Nonprofit (since 1986) that runs Japanese-language debate tournaments. Use for: building and defending arguments under pressure. Note: debate rewards winning, not truth-seeking.
- [Foundation for Critical Thinking: events and online community](https://www.criticalthinking.org/)
  Paid workshops (e.g., "Artificial Intelligence & Critical Thinking", December 2026) and an online community with a free trial. Use for: structured training in the Paul–Elder framework, if budget allows.
- [LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/)
  Strong on reasoning and forecasting, but now dominated by AI-safety topics and heavy jargon. Use for: reading, not as a first community.

## Gaps

- **Effect of AI use on thinking skills over time.** The widely shared "Your Brain on ChatGPT" study ([arXiv 2506.08872](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.08872)) is a small (54 people), not peer-reviewed preprint with published criticism ([arXiv 2601.00856](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.00856)). Don't cite it as settled.
- **Evidence for AI "Socratic partner" protocols.** No strong study yet on which rally prompts improve a person's own reasoning. Our rally rules come from the sycophancy and confidence findings above, plus checklist design.
- **Ataka's book text.** Verify the three conditions of a good issue and the issue-degree × answer-quality matrix against the book (ask whether the user owns it).

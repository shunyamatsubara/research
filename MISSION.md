# Mission: A checklist that tells me if my thinking is good

## Why
I learn new fields fast (AI, robotics, new tools and papers) and I write to explain and argue. Today I am flooded with input, I find hidden assumptions too late, and my analysis often ends without a clear "so what". I want a short checklist that shows whether my thinking is productive and effective, and a way to use it in a back-and-forth with AI, so that each round makes my thinking better, not just longer.

## Success looks like
- Before I read about a new topic, I write the one question that matters and what it is for, and I use it to drop most of the input.
- In any draft, I can list the 3–5 assumptions that carry the conclusion and mark each one as checked, untested, or borrowed from an analogy.
- I end any analysis with a 2–3 sentence "so what": the conclusion, what it changes, and what would change my mind.
- I have my own one-page checklist (9 items or fewer) and a set of AI prompts that make the AI critique my thinking instead of flattering it.
- I can run a 3-round AI rally on a real draft and show what got better in each round.

## Constraints
- 10–15 minutes per lesson.
- English is not my first language: plain words and short sentences.
- I already know MECE, logic trees, and common cognitive biases.

## Out of scope
- Personal life and money decisions (not chosen as a focus in the first interview; revisit if that changes).
- Formal logic and philosophy for their own sake: only what the checklist needs.

Ontology for Agent Decisions

A short course for judging tools that say an “ontology” lets AI agents decide reliably from messy input. Running example: health-insurance claim review.

Your mission

Vendors, Palantir first, say an ontology makes agent decisions reliable, even deterministic. You want to tell real engineering from marketing, so you can advise on or choose such a tool. By the end you should be able to:

  • draw from memory where determinism lives in an “agent + ontology” system, and where it does not;
  • explain how a Palantir-style ontology (objects, links, actions) differs from a semantic-web ontology (OWL classes and reasoning);
  • score a vendor demo or docs page with a short list of sharp questions;
  • build a tiny claim reviewer in Python: a model reads, the facts are checked, a rule table decides.

Lessons

  1. 01 Where determinism lives 12 min The read step, the decide step, and the ontology as the contract between them. Includes a claim simulator.
  2. 02What an ontology adds beyond a schemaPlanned Is-a inheritance, constraints, and why a rule written once can cover many cases.
  3. 03Two meanings of “ontology”Planned Palantir’s objects, links and actions next to OWL’s classes and reasoning. Open world and closed world.
  4. 04Checking the read stepPlanned Validation before deciding, “unknown” as an answer, and when a person must look.
  5. 05Python lab: a tiny claim reviewerPlanned Build the whole pipeline yourself and break each part on purpose.
  6. 06The vendor scorecardPlanned Judge a real product page with the questions you built along the way.

The plan changes as you learn. Later lessons depend on what you find easy or hard.

How to use this course

  • One lesson at a time. Each takes about 10 to 15 minutes.
  • Answer practice questions from memory before you look back. The effort is what makes it stick.
  • Bring questions, and your written answers, to the chat with your teacher.
  • Each new lesson opens with a few questions from earlier ones.

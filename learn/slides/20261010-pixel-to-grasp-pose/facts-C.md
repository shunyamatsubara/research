## B. Depth/sensor error sources

| id | statement | source | date | reliability | notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| C24 | ClearGrasp abstract: transparent objects have visual properties that make them "incredibly difficult for standard 3D sensors" and depth often appears as "noisy or distorted approximations of the surfaces that lie behind them." | https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.02550 | 2019-10 | primary | For a transparent bottle, the back-projected point can land on the table behind it. |
| C25 | AnyGrasp's authors excluded objects with "a large portion of transparent or black surface" because depth sensors read them poorly, and say the RGB-based alternative is "not fully addressed yet." | https://arxiv.org/html/2212.08333 (experiments/limitations) | 2022-12 (v2 2023-06) | primary | Even dedicated grasp networks inherit the depth hole problem. |
| C26 | Time-of-flight pixels at depth edges integrate light from foreground and background and report an in-between "flying pixel" depth that floats in empty space in the point cloud. | https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2021/papers/Chugunov_Mask-ToF_Learning_Microlens_Masks_for_Flying_Pixel_Correction_in_Time-of-Flight_CVPR_2021_paper.pdf (via search summary) | 2021 | secondary | Read through a search-engine summary, not the PDF. Supports "a VLM point on an object edge gives a wrong depth". Stereo cameras have different edge artifacts; a stereo-specific source was not found. |
| C27 | A peer-reviewed paper reports RealSense depth images often have unfilled regions that can span both background and foreground objects. | Cho, Park, Chien, IEEE Access vol 8, pp 53901-53914 (2020), https://research.knu.ac.kr/en/publications/hole-filling-of-realsense-depth-images-using-a-color-edge-map/ | 2020 | secondary | Via search summary. |
| C28 | An Intel community answer says reflective surfaces and screen reflections can produce black (invalid) regions in RealSense depth and suggests manual exposure/gain. | https://communities.intel.com/thread/126992 | 2018 | hearsay | Forum reply from a moderator; no official Intel doc on align-to-color was reachable (fetch of dev.intelrealsense.com failed). |
| C29 | Contact-GraspNet takes a raw single-view depth image (optionally masks), targets "an only partially observable scene", notes self-occlusion makes the grasp distribution ambiguous, and does not evaluate calibration error or discuss transparent objects. | https://arxiv.org/html/2103.14127 | 2021-03 | primary | Absences are as extracted. A grasp network sees only the visible front surface. |
| C30 | AnyGrasp says depth noise up to about +-5 mm still worked in its bin test; its camera-to-robot extrinsics came from ArUco marker detection plus manual measurement. | https://arxiv.org/html/2212.08333 | 2022-12 | primary | Gives a scale: sensor noise of mm, versus calibration error of cm in OK-Robot-like setups (not directly quantified there). |
| C31 | AnyGrasp's dynamic-grasp setup used an eye-in-hand RealSense L515 because the robot can occlude an overhead camera during tracking and the L515 works at close range where D415/D435 cannot produce depth. | https://arxiv.org/html/2212.08333 | 2022-12 | primary | Eye-in-hand brings a minimum depth range: the close-range view that would help closed-loop fails on some cameras. |

## Facts bearing directly on the thesis (stage -> error, absorbable by closed loop?)

- Stage 1, VLM output: 2D only, no confidence (C9), normalized 0-1000 [y,x] (C1), no orientation beyond top-down in the Gemini report (C5), authors' own hedges on 3D understanding (C13, C16, C17). A closed-loop policy does not need this stage's metric precision if the 3D target is only "near the object" (inference from C37, C39; not directly sourced).
- Stage 2, point or mask to a surface pixel: edge points and boxes include background (C7), flying pixels at depth edges (C26), holes/background depth on transparent, black, reflective objects (C24, C25, C28). These errors can be tens of cm (point falls on the table behind) rather than cm; no source quantifies this, and a closed-loop policy absorbing them is unsourced.
- Stage 3, back-projection: needs depth registered to color (C11) and intrinsics; the point is the visible surface, not the grasp center, so systems add an offset (C8) or a grasp generator (C12, C15).
- Stage 4, orientation: comes from a grasp generator or a fixed rule, not the VLM (C5, C12, C15, C42); generators see only the visible front surface (C19, C29).
- Stage 5, camera-to-base transform: calibration drift reported as a real failure source (C21), calibration quality differs by cm between methods (C34); a better VLM does not reduce it (inference). Eye-in-hand removes the fixed-camera occlusion by the arm (C31) but the gripper can occlude the target when close (C32).
- Compounding: OK-Robot's "multiplicative error accumulation" (C22).
- What closed loop absorbs: sources state qualitatively that learned closed-loop policies correct disturbances and recover from mistakes (C37, C39) and the gain over open-loop grasp detection is larger when initial poses are suboptimal (C38). No source found giving a numeric tolerance (cm) for the handoff (C41). Learned policies are themselves sensitive to camera viewpoint shifts (C35, C36), so a calibration error does not vanish; it moves to the policy's input.

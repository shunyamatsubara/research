## 2. Depth accuracy

| id | statement | source | date | reliability | notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| B7 | Intel's depth RMS error model: Depth RMS error (mm) = Distance(mm)^2 x Subpixel / (focal length(px) x Baseline(mm)), with focal length(px) = Xres / (2 tan(HFOV/2)). | Intel BKM tuning PDF (URL in B3), "Understand theoretical limit" | about 2018-2019 | primary | Error grows with the SQUARE of distance. "For a well-textured target you can expect to see Subpixel < 0.1 and even approaching 0.05." About 30% smaller subpixel with the laser off. |
| B8 | Worked example (computed): D435 at 848x480, HFOV 90 deg (f=424 px), baseline 50 mm, subpixel 0.08 gives RMS 0.34 mm at 0.3 m, 0.94 mm at 0.5 m, 3.8 mm at 1 m, 15 mm at 2 m. | Computed from B7 with the example parameters Intel itself gives (D435 HFOV=90, Xres=848, baseline=50, subpixel=0.08) | - | computed | 15 mm at 2 m = 0.75%, consistent with the "<2% at 2 m" spec (B9). D415 (HFOV 65, Xres 1280, baseline 55): 0.36 / 1.45 / 5.8 mm at 0.5 / 1 / 2 m. |
| B9 | Intel's support article lists Z-accuracy as <=2% for D400 through D435i, up to 2 m and 80% of the field of view (source cited: Table 4-9 of the D400 datasheet). | https://intel.com/content/www/us/en/support/articles/000030916.html (search snippet; Intel datasheet itself not reachable) | 2021-07-07 reviewed | primary (via snippet) | **[DIFFERS]** One Intel community thread says another Intel page says <1%. 2% of 2 m = 40 mm. This is a spec ceiling; B8 shows noise is far below it near 0.5 m, but this figure includes bias. |
| B10 | D435 has more than 2x the depth noise of D415 at any given range, but a smaller minimum distance. | Intel BKM PDF (B3): "the D435 has more depth noise (>2x) at any given range, compared to the D415." | about 2018-2019 | primary | |
| B11 | Minimum depth (MinZ) formula: MinZ(mm) = focal length(px) x baseline(mm) / 126; example "D415 at 1280x720 MinZ about 43.8 cm, D435 at 848x480 about 16.8 cm". | Intel BKM PDF (B3) | about 2018-2019 | primary | **[DIFFERS]** The same PDF later says "at 848x480 the D415 has a minZ of about 31cm, whereas the D435 has a minZ of about 19.5cm"; a search snippet of a 2019 Intel forum answer says 11 cm (D435) and 16 cm (D415); a reseller says about 28 cm. | B12 | D405 (short-range, 7-50 cm): "ideal range of 7 cm to 50 cm"; resellers' copies of the spec sheet say "Depth accuracy: +/- 2% at 50cm"; Intel marketing says "sub-millimeter accuracy". | https://intelrealsense.com/depth-camera-d405/ and reseller pages (https://www.mybotshop.de/Intel-RealSense-Depth-Camera-D405_1), seen via search snippets only | unknown | secondary (could not open Intel page) | **[DIFFERS]** Minimum object detection at 7 cm: "500 microns" (Intel store), "<1 mm" (product page), "0.1 mm" (distributor). | B13 | A hand-eye paper states the depth accuracy of a 3D camera at the working distance is "up to 3 mm" and that this significantly affects calibration results; another says D435 depth accuracy at 0.3-0.8 m is roughly 10 mm. | https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.01335 (snippet); https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.19851 (snippet) | 2023 / 2025 | secondary | **[DIFFERS]** 3 mm vs about 10 mm vs B8's 0.3-0.9 mm noise: noise (precision) and absolute error (bias, surface, angle) are different. ## 3. VLM pointing

| id | statement | source | date | reliability | notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| B15 | Molmo outputs points as plain text with coordinates "normalized between 0 and 100"; the format is `<point x="10.0" y="10.0" alt="...">`. Its vision encoder is OpenAI ViT-L/14 336px; the image is tiled into square 336x336 crops (about 12 high-res crops + 1 low-res, max 13), so the model sees a downscaled version of the image. | https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.17146 (Molmo paper, text extracted locally: Section 2 Architecture and Appendix, "Pointing" paragraph) | 2024-09 | primary | Molmo's own table 11 reports counting precision/recall/F1 (about 75%); I did not find point-in-mask accuracy for Molmo in that paper. |
| B16 | Gemini Robotics-ER returns points as `[{"point": [y, x], "label": ...}]` in [y, x] order "normalized to 0-1000"; example output `{"point": [376, 508], ...}`. | https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/robotics-overview | accessed 2026-10 | primary | Order is y then x (opposite to Molmo and RoboPoint). | B17 | RoboPoint points are image coordinates "normalized between 0 and 1", written (x, y). | https://arxiv.org/html/2406.10721v1 | 2024-06 | primary | Three VLMs, three conventions: (x,y) 0-1, (x,y) 0-100, (y,x) 0-1000. A frequent bug source. |
| B18 | Success on RoboRefIt / Where2Place / Where2Place(h) is the "percentage of predicted points within the target mask": RoboPoint 49.82 / 46.77 / 44.48; GPT-4o 15.28 / 29.06 / 27.14; Qwen-VL 24.08 / 10.49 / 9.90; LLaVA-NeXT-34B 19.91 / 15.02 / 14.76. | https://arxiv.org/html/2406.10721v1 Table 2 | 2024-06 | primary | **[DIFFERS]** A 2025 third-party paper (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.10912) reports Where2Place GPT-4o 23.04% and RoboPoint-13B 46.80% (snippet). RoboRefIt = 750-image subset; Where2Place = 100 real images. So even the best of these pointing models misses the mask about half the time on these tests. RoboPoint reports no latency. |
| B19 | Point-Bench (about 1,000 tasks, five categories): a point counts if it lies within a target mask and every target region is covered. Molmo-72B scored highest, 0.43 points above Gemini-2.5-Pro ("statistically insignificant"). Average scores in Table 1: GPT-4o 29.5%, Gemini-2.5-Flash 46.8%; Qwen2.5-VL-7B 52.3% after adding PixMo data vs Qwen2-VL-7B 17.4%; LLaVA variants 4.8-17.4%. | https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09990v1 (PointArena); https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.09990 | 2025-05 | primary (via fetch summary; I did not see the full results figure) | Chain-of-thought lowered pointing accuracy by 2.9% for GPT-4o and 16% for Gemini-2.5-Flash. Point-Bench vs real-robot Point-Act correlation R^2 = 0.92. A later Molmo2 paper lists Point-Bench overall Molmo2-8B 68.7, Molmo2-4B 67.7, Molmo-7B-O 63.3 (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.10611, snippet, columns not verified). |
| B20 | Gemini Robotics report: large VLMs such as Gemini Robotics-ER are slow ("Inference in large VLMs ... is often slow and requires special hardware"); the distilled backbone's "query-to-response latency has been optimized from seconds to under 160ms", end-to-end about 250 ms. | https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.20020 (snippet) | 2025-03 | primary (snippet) | So an un-distilled VLM query is "seconds"; no exact seconds-per-pointing-query figure found. Use "seconds per query, once per grasp, not per control step". |

Note on scale: the pointing mask test is "inside the mask", not "within X mm". A point anywhere on a 6 cm mug counts as correct, but as a grasp centre it can be 3 cm off the true centre (computed from object width, not from a paper).

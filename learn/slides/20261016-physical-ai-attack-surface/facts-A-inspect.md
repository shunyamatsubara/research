# facts-A-inspect: AI visual inspection and AI-driven process control in automotive parts plants

Angle A (mechanism). Collected 2026-10-16. Read only brief.md in the working folder.

Verification: every row was checked against its cited source on 2026-10-16 by an independent checker; see verify-A-inspect.md. Rows with changed statements: A314, A325, A333, A344, A346, A356, A359. Rows with a notes-only fix: A309, A319, A320, A324.

Method note (read before quoting):
- Most pages were read with a fetch tool that passes the page through a small model. Text in quotation marks in the notes column is what that tool returned as quoted text; it may not be letter-perfect. Re-check against the page before printing any quote verbatim.
- Two sources were PDFs that I extracted myself with pdftotext, so their quotes are verbatim: the Bosch VQI white paper (rows A303, A304, A322, A327, A328; PDF creation date 2021-08-23, no date printed) and the Denso Kyushu / Kyutech news release (row A349).
- Reliability: primary = the originating company, standards body or paper; secondary = a third party describing it (including a software vendor's customer story); hearsay = none used.
- Tags in notes: [factory] [product] [supply-chain] [general]. This angle is almost entirely [factory] and [supply-chain]; no row is a shipped-product fact.
- Web search budget ran out partway through; later gaps are listed at the end.

## Station sequence (assembled from the rows above; no step is added that no row supports)

1. Present and image the part. The part is placed or held in front of a camera: a five-megapixel top-down camera (A301), or a robot holding the part in a lighting dome (A302). Camera and lighting are sized to the smallest defect and to keep stray light out (A304).
2. Pre-process and validate input. Images pass pre-processing and, in the Bosch VQI design, data checks for file validity and label presence at ingestion (A303, A328).
3. Run the learned model. Three families appear: classification into OK/NOK (A302, A303), anomaly detection that scores how far an image is from a stored notion of "good" (A305, A309, A311, A315), and pixel-level maps or masks that show where (A308, A313). The feature extractor is often pre-trained outside the plant (A310, A303).
4. Turn a score into a verdict with a threshold. The anomaly score is compared with a predefined threshold: above it, reject (A307). The threshold is picked on validation data, with F1-optimal or manual values (A312, A313, A314); good-images-only training leaves no defects to pick it on (A314, A315).
5. Act on the verdict. The result is sent to the machine as a digital signal to a PLC (A322) or as an MQTT message (A308); a fail leads to rejection (A307), to a human reviewer who sees only flagged cases (A317, A318), to a red screen for the operator (A319), or to an immediate process adjustment (A320, A341).
6. Log the result. The VQI chain ends in "Display and Logging" (A303); the OPC UA machine-vision standard moves results with product assignment, times and status in a manufacturer-specific format (A324); the MES keeps quality and traceability records (A326). No public source ties an AI inspection verdict to a named MES record.
7. Train, change and roll out the model. Training uses good images only (A306, A315) or labelled defect images, with synthetic images and a shared foundation model to speed up (A329 to A336); labels sit in a central repository across sites (A327); the model is retrained weekly in one plant (A319), by operators in another product (A321), with drift alerts (A328); the vision job travels as an opaque recipe (A323); Bosch targets weeks instead of 6 to 12 months per rollout (A329) and reports roughly 50 plants on one anomaly-detection software (A342).

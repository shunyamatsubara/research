Method: every cited source was fetched again (arXiv PDFs and abstract pages, NIST, Keen Lab, Trend Micro, EU Publications Office XHTML, vendor and government pages) and the quoted passages were searched in the text I extracted myself. Verdicts: confirmed = I read the supporting passage and numbers/names/dates match; corrected = the source says something different or the row overclaims or a date or standard has changed; replaced-source, unsupported, unreachable = none were needed.

## Other observations (not changes to rows)

- C127 to C129 and key fact 5: the EU Machinery Regulation applies from 20 January 2027. The originally published Art. 54 text says 14 January 2027; the corrigendum of 4 July 2023 changed it (together with 13 other dates). Reg. (EU) 2026/1744 (Digital Omnibus on AI, signed 8 July 2026, OJ 24 July 2026, in force 27 July 2026) amends the Machinery Regulation by adding delegated-act powers for AI requirements in Annex III (to apply by 2 August 2028) and a presumption of conformity via AI Act standards; it leaves Annex I Part A items 5-6, Annex III 1.1.9 and 1.2.1 and Art. 54 untouched.
- The Publications Office URL https://publications.europa.eu/resource/celex/32023R1230 returns RDF metadata by default; send "Accept: application/xhtml+xml" and "Accept-Language: eng" to get the text.
- NIST AI 100-2 E2025 is still the current edition (page shows Final, March 2025, an errata note of 2025-06-03, no E2026).
- C113 is the only row where two sources disagree (project page vs press coverage on the stop-sign duration); I could not read the CCS PDF.

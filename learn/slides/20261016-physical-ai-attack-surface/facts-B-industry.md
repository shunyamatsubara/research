Prepared 2026-10-16 for the article "Where can an attacker get in when a learned model decides what a robot arm, an AMR or a car does". Angle B, sub-topic: every real number a reader needs to feel the size of the client's world.

How to read the table
- date = date of the source (publication or last revision) or, for vendor pages without a date, "undated, read 2026-10-16".
- reliability: primary = the publisher's own page, the legal text or the paper itself; secondary = a second-hand compilation; hearsay = unattributed claim; derived = arithmetic done by me on the cited rows (not a sourced fact, shown only so the reader can feel a size).
- notes carry the exact quote behind each number, then a tag: [factory], [product], [supply-chain] or [general]. "DIFFERS" marks a number that differs between sources.
- Statement text is fact only; interpretation is kept in notes and marked "Reading:".

Retrieval notes (what could and could not be read)
- WebSearch was not available in this run (the search budget was already used up). I did not use any search engine or archive service. All pages below were fetched directly by URL (WebFetch, or curl for pages WebFetch could not render), and every quoted figure was checked against the fetched page text or PDF text, not against a search snippet.

The legal texts (Machinery Regulation, AI Act, AI Omnibus, CRA, GSR, UN R155/R156 as printed in the OJ) were read as official OJ XHTML from the EU Publications Office (publications.europa.eu/resource/celex/...). These are the as-published texts, not consolidated versions.

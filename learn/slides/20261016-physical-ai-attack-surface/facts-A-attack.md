# facts-A-attack: attack mechanisms on learned models (angle A)

Compiled 2026-10-16. Question served: when a learned model decides what a robot arm, an AMR or a car does, where can an attacker get in, and which of the supplier's controls can see each way in. This file covers only the attack methods themselves, as mechanisms acting on a learned model.

How to read the table
- One row per fact. Statements are plain facts taken from the cited source; interpretation is kept in notes and labelled "Reading:".
- Quotes in notes are verbatim from the page or PDF text I read (fetch tool or pdftotext). WebFetch limits quotes to short phrases, so most quotes are fragments.
- Tags in notes: [factory] = lands on the factory floor, [product] = lands in the shipped product (ECU, vehicle, robot), [supply-chain] = lands in the model and data supply chain, [general] = applies to all.
- "Shown:" in notes says the highest level of evidence the source itself reports: digital only / simulation / lab / real vehicle or real robot / seen in the wild.
- reliability: primary = the original paper, vendor or standards page itself. Vendor research blogs are marked primary for what the vendor found, with the caveat in notes.
- Families: rows A401-A415 adversarial examples and patches; A416-A425 sensor spoofing and jamming; A426-A433 poisoning and backdoors; A434-A442 language-instructed robots and VLA models; A443-A454 model-file and ML supply chain; A455-A458 model extraction and membership inference.

| A407 | Thys et al. generated an adversarial patch (printable on a cardboard plate and held in front of a person) that, applied digitally to INRIA test images, cut YOLOv2's person recall from 100% (clean baseline) to 26.46% for the best patch (OBJ); random noise of the same size left it at 87.14%. [factory] [product] verified; corrected 2026-10-16: the earlier statement implied the 26.46% was measured with a printed plate held by a person; PDF Sec. 4 says patches were evaluated "by applying them to the Inria test set using the same process we used during training", the real-world test (Fig. 7) reports no recall figure, and the 26.46% is at the clean PR-curve working point (threshold 0.4). |

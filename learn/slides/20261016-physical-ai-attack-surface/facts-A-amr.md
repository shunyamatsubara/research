## Step sequence: how an AMR gets from a mission to motion

This is my assembly of the rows below. Each step names the rows it rests on. "Classical" means fixed algorithms with no trained model, as far as the cited sources say. "Not published" means I looked and the vendor documents do not give it.

**S0. Offline: map, layout, zones (once, then updated).**
- Inputs: an operator drives or teaches the vehicle through the site; a layout of routes, stations, zones.
- Operation: laser SLAM builds the map; the fleet control holds the full node/edge graph and the zone set. VDA 5050 has an operating mode TEACH_IN in which "mapping is done by an operator" (A116).
- Outputs: a map (mapId + mapVersion), a route graph, zones.
- Rate: once, then changes by operator or by "Live Map" updates from vehicles (A113, A123).
- Learned or classical: classical (A118, A146).

**S1. Sense (continuous, on the vehicle).**
- Inputs: 2D safety laser scanner(s) (nanoScan3: 275 degrees, 3 m protective field); wheel odometry (Rexroth: optional add-on to the laser); stereo or RGB-D cameras for 3D obstacles; 8 proximity sensors on the MiR250 (A121, A135, A140, A118).
- Operation: raw range and depth measurement.
- Outputs: laser scans, wheel ticks, depth images.
- Rate: safety scanner response time 70 ms (A140); NAV350 reflector lidar scanning frequency 8 Hz (A150). Vendor documents for ROKIT Locator, ActiveShuttle and MiR250 do not give scan or camera frame rates (not published).
- Learned or classical: classical measurement.

**S2. Localise (continuous, on the vehicle).**
- Inputs: laser scan, odometry, the stored map.
- Operation, one of: natural-feature laser SLAM (ROKIT Locator, ActiveShuttle, BMW STR); reflector triangulation (NAV350); floor Data Matrix/QR grid read by a downward camera (GLS6); magnetic tape; a "blue line" accessory whose physical form the documents do not describe (A118, A121, A128, A146, A150, A151, A152). Open-source reference: particle filter that scores the scan against the map, fused with wheel-encoder and IMU data (A147).
- Outputs: pose x, y, theta on a mapId, a `localized` flag, an optional `localizationScore` (A110).
- Rate: NAV350 reflector lidar scans at 8 Hz (no pose output rate in its data sheet); the Nav2 survey says localization "typically occurs at a reduced rate" relative to state estimation (A147, A150). ROKIT Locator rate not published.
- Learned or classical: classical in every vendor stack I could read (A118, A121, A135, A146).

**S3. Mission in: a transport request becomes a VDA 5050 order.**
- Inputs: a transport request from MES/ERP/PLC/dash button/tablet, or a REST call (A123, A130).
- Operation: the fleet manager (Rexroth AMS, now ACTIVE Fleet Manager; MiR Fleet) assigns the order to a vehicle, computes the route on its graph, handles traffic and deadlocks (A117, A123, A126).
- Outputs: JSON `order` with nodes, edges, actions; `released` marks the base, the rest is horizon (A107, A108).
- Rate: event-driven; the robot's factsheet carries a minimum order interval (A111).
- Learned or classical: classical (assignment, graph search). Denso reports using a D-Wave annealer for AGV routing in 2018, also not a learned model (A131).

**S4. Transport: MQTT over Wi-Fi or private 5G.**
- Inputs: the order JSON.
- Operation: published to the broker on topic `.../order`, QoS 0 (best effort); the robot publishes `state` back; a last-will message on `connection` signals loss (A102, A103, A115).
- Outputs: order on the vehicle; state at the fleet manager.
- Rate: state "when relevant events occur or at least every 30 seconds" (A110).
- Security: the specification leaves it to broker configuration (A104, A105). Rexroth lists Wi-Fi encryption options WEP to WPA2 Enterprise (A122).
- Learned or classical: classical.

**S5. Plan on the vehicle: from nodes to a drivable path.**
- Inputs: next released nodes/edges, pose, local obstacle map.
- Operation: line-guided robots follow the given edges; freely navigating robots plan their own trajectory but "shall follow" a trajectory in the order; an optional corridor allows deviation to avoid obstacles (A109). Rexroth ROKIT Navigator: "Virtual Line Following", optional avoidance "within the permitted avoidance area" (A119).
- Outputs: velocity commands (vx, omega).
- Rate: open-source reference: controller 20 Hz default, behaviour-tree tick 100 Hz, replanning "typically 5-30s" (A148). ROKIT Navigator rate not published.
- Learned or classical: classical in the documented vendor stacks. A learned end-to-end policy exists as research (X-Mobility, A158).

**S6. Perceive obstacles for planning (not for safety).**
- Inputs: laser scan; depth from stereo or RGB-D cameras.
- Operation: scan and depth go into a costmap or obstacle map; some stacks add a learned layer that finds people (NVIDIA nvblox with a person-segmentation network in its people-reconstruction mode) or pallets (MiR1200) (A124, A149, A153, A156).
- Outputs: a 2D costmap with obstacles and, optionally, "people" cells.
- Rate: nvblox identifies obstacles up to five meters away and updates "in under 300 ms"; Isaac Perceptor processes depth at 30 Hz per camera (A155).
- Learned or classical: mixed. Laser obstacle layer: classical. Stereo-camera obstacle detection in ActiveShuttle: algorithm not stated. Person segmentation in Isaac Perceptor, pallet detection in MiR1200, BMW/NVIDIA 2020 pilot: learned (A153, A154, A156, A157).

**S7. Protect: the safety laser scanner stops the vehicle, independently of S2 to S6.**
- Inputs: the scanner's own scan; the active protective field (selected by speed or load via static or dynamic field switching).
- Operation: if an object enters the protective field, the scanner switches its OSSD outputs off ("Safe state in the event of a fault: At least one OSSD is in the OFF state", A140); the vehicle's safety control then has to bring the truck to a stop. ISO 3691-4 requires the truck to "stop before contact" with a stationary person; the personnel-detection function needs PLr d (A142, A143; the TUV source covers the 2020 edition). The OSSD-to-brake wiring is vehicle-specific and not documented in the sources I read.
- Outputs: OSSD off (safe state), reported upward as `safetyState.fieldViolation` (A110).
- Rate: response time 70 ms plus a 65 mm protective-field supplement (A140).
- Learned or classical: classical. ISO/TR 22100-5 states that it "does not address safety systems with AI, for example, safety-related sensors" (A145). The scanner standard IEC 61496-3 is written for near-infrared diffuse-reflection scanners (A144).
- Caveat: the scanner is configured by PC software over USB, and on the same device a non-safe measured-data output feeds navigation (A140, A141). In the 2021 MiR advisory the safety PLC was reachable over the network with a default password (A138).

**S8. Act: velocity to wheels.**
- Inputs: velocity command, safety state.
- Operation: drive controller and motor (Rexroth: ROKIT Motor module, ctrlX CORE as vehicle control, ctrlX SAFETY as safety control) (A119, A120).
- Rate: not published.
- Learned or classical: classical.

**S9. Report and update.**
- Inputs: pose, order progress, errors, safety state.
- Operation: `state` message; fleet manager extends the base as the vehicle nears the decision point (`newBaseRequest`); maps, zone sets and certificates are pushed as instant actions (the robot pulls files from a URL) (A108, A112, A113).
- Learned or classical: classical.

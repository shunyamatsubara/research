The locomotion stack

Four layers, their status in 2026, and how to judge a startup’s claim at each one.

Layer What it is Status Key evidence Where a moat could be
L4 Whole-body loco-manipulation Arms and legs working together while walking; staying safe near people Open Frontier is “dexterous interaction” (Frey 2026); safety standard ISO 25785-1 still a draft Safety record and certification; real interaction data
L3 Navigation and autonomy Where to go and whether it is safe, with no map, GPS or marked path Open Frontier is “semantic understanding” (Frey 2026); Field AI’s whole pitch Field hours, intervention rate, data from hard sites, fleet operations
L2 Perceptive locomotion Cameras or LiDAR used to place each foot Maturing 2.2 km mountain hike in 78 min, but snow, grass and water fool the sensors (Miki 2022) Perception that holds up in bad conditions; field data
L1 Gait control Speed command to joint targets, body sensors only, firm ground Near-commodity Flat ground in under 4 min on one GPU (Rudin 2021); humanoids in under 30 min (MuJoCo Playground); Go2 from $1,600 Not in the walking policy. If anywhere, in hardware cost and sales volume.

Two questions for any claim

  1. Which layer is it? L1 on firm ground: assume a public recipe can match it.
  2. What evidence goes beyond a demo?

Strong evidence

  • Field hours, and how often a human had to step in
  • Falls per hour or per km, measured over months
  • Paying customers who come back for more robots
  • Data from sites that others cannot reach

Weak evidence

  • A viral video or a race win
  • Training speed (“walks after 20 minutes”)
  • The size of the model or the reward
  • A long list of robot bodies it “supports”

The two common arguments, in one line each

  • “The reward is simple.” The task reward is; the style reward is not (Peng 2021). Real setups use 9 active terms for a quadruped and 15 to 16 for a humanoid, tuned per robot. A cost, not a moat.
  • “It is easy to simulate.” The rigid body is. Motors (Hwangbo 2019), sensors (Agarwal 2022) and soft ground (Choi 2023) are not. Still far easier than manipulation (Goldberg 2025).

Field AI in one paragraph

Field AI sells L3: risk-aware navigation “without prior maps, GPS, or predefined paths,” across many robot bodies (2025 release). On Spot, Boston Dynamics does the walking and Field AI does the deciding (2026 release). It is hiring to build its own locomotion for humanoids (job post). Its numbers are company claims; no independent field data is public.

Words from lesson 0001

  • Gait control: the lowest layer, turning a speed command into joint targets from body sensors.
  • Perceptive locomotion: walking that uses cameras or LiDAR to choose where each foot goes.
  • Task reward vs style reward: the part that says what to achieve vs the many terms that say how to move.
  • Actuator network: a neural network that learns how the real motors respond, because they are hard to model by hand.
  • Teacher–student training: train a “teacher” with perfect simulator data, then copy its behavior into a “student” that only has real sensors.
  • Intervention: a moment when a human must step in to help the robot; the rate per hour is a key field metric.

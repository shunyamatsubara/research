DGE at a Glance

Who Diamond Global Energy is, where its LNG comes from, who buys it, and what is still unknown. Public sources only; numbers in brackets point to the source list.

In one paragraph

DGE is Mitsubishi Corporation’s LNG marketing and trading company in Singapore, fully owned by MC. It sells the LNG from MC’s rights in two North American plants, about 4 million tonnes a year from Cameron LNG (US) and 2.1 million tonnes a year from LNG Canada, mostly to Japanese utilities on long-term deals, and trades extra cargoes around that supply. Until 15 March 2026 it was called Diamond Gas International (DGI).

What it does, in its own words

  • MC: “Based in Singapore, Diamond Global Energy Pte. Ltd. is engaged in the marketing of LNG produced by Mitsubishi Corporation-affiliated projects in the United States and Canada, as well as LNG sales optimization and short-term trading.” [2]
  • DGE’s employer profile: it “specializes in Marketing, Trading and Shipping of LNG. As a marketing arm of all the equity LNG which Mitsubishi Corporation will be entitled to, DGE is also aggressively working to find new markets for LNG.” [7]
  • Conference profile (Feb 2026, as DGI): it “also manages commercial contracts, oversees execution, and ensures smooth delivery operations. Serving a broad global customer base—from major Japanese utilities to international trading firms”. [8]

What this suggests (to check with DGE)

The public LNG Canada deals add up to its full 2.1 Mt/yr, all to Japanese buyers. The public Cameron deals add up to only about 0.6 of 4.0 Mt/yr. Either other contracts are not public, or a large part of the Cameron supply is sold on short-term or spot terms, which would leave it more open to price swings. Many deals are “delivered to the buyer’s port”, so DGE carries the shipping risk on those cargoes.

HOA (heads of agreement): a preliminary deal. SPA (sales and purchase agreement): the final contract. Public sources do not confirm whether the LNG Canada HOAs became SPAs.

What the parent wants

  • More LNG: MC plans to raise its equity LNG capacity from about 15 to about 18 million tonnes a year in the early 2030s (it was 14.90 in July 2025) [25]. The LNG Canada expansion is part of that [5].
  • More US gas: MC closed its $7.5 billion purchase of Aethon’s US gas fields and pipelines in July 2026 [26]. This is gas that could one day feed LNG.
  • Options on LNG Canada: in January 2026, Reuters reported that MC had hired RBC Capital Markets to weigh options for its 15% stake [24]. In September 2026 MC approved the expansion instead [5].

For DGE this means more LNG to sell in the 2030s. Lesson 2 asks whether there will be buyers for it at a good spread.

How it trades and ships

  • Trading: joined Platts’ daily price-setting window for Asian LNG (the JKM “Market on Close”) in 2018 and the European one in 2020 [16]. It placed its first bids in the Atlantic window on 16 Feb 2026 [15], and asked to join the Asian and Atlantic windows again under its new name in April 2026 [16].
  • Ships: long-term charters include Diamond Gas Crystal and Diamond Gas Victoria (2021) [19], Diamond Gas Jade (named May 2026, for LNG Canada) [17] and Diamond Gas Nexus (174,000 m³, named Sep 2026, mainly for Cameron) [18].
  • Systems: an ETRM system (energy trading and risk management software) for the Cameron business went live in 2018, built by Publicis Sapient for DGI and MC Global Gas Corp. in Houston. The product is not named [20].

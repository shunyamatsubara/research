First, what LNG is

Natural gas is hard to carry across an ocean. So it is cooled to −162 °C (−260 °F). At that temperature it turns into a liquid that takes up 600 times less space. This liquid is LNG, liquefied natural gas. Special ships carry it in super-cold tanks. At the other end, an import terminal warms it back into gas.SourceUS Energy Information Administration, “Liquefied natural gas explained” [1].

One cargo’s journey

  1. Gas field. Gas comes out of wells in the US or Canada and travels by pipeline to the plant.
  2. LNG plant. TODO-PLANTS
  3. LNG ship. One shipload is one cargo. A standard cargo holds about 3.4 million MMBtu of energy.WordMMBtu: one million British thermal units, a unit of energy. Gas and LNG prices are quoted in US dollars per MMBtu.SourceThe JKM price standard uses cargoes of 3.3–3.5 trillion Btu, that is 3.3–3.5 million MMBtu (S&P Global Platts, February 2026) [3].
  4. Import terminal. The ship unloads in the buyer’s country, and the LNG is warmed back into gas.
  5. Buyer. A power company, a city gas company or another trader uses or resells the gas, and pays for the cargo.

How DGE makes money: the spread

For each cargo, the money that stays is the sale price minus all the costs of getting the LNG to the buyer. In this course we call it the spread.

sale pricewhat the buyer pays for the cargo
−gas costUS plants: about 115% of the Henry Hub price
−plant feea fixed fee for turning gas into LNG and loading it
−ship costpaid when the seller delivers the cargo
=spreadUS dollars per MMBtu

For US plants, a common estimate of the gas cost is 115% of the Henry Hub price. The extra 15% pays for the fuel the plant burns, pipeline losses and on-site power. On top of that, the company taking the LNG pays a fee for liquefaction, storage and loading that rises with inflation.WordHenry Hub: the main US natural gas price, set at a pipeline hub in Louisiana.SourceEIA, “Ten years after first Sabine Pass cargo, U.S. LNG exports are still on the rise”, 24 February 2026 [2].

The sale price depends on the contract. It can follow JKM, the spot price for LNG delivered to Japan, South Korea, China and Taiwan. It can follow the price of oil: many long-term contracts set the LNG price at 12–13% of the Brent oil price. Some contracts even follow Henry Hub, which passes the price risk to the buyer. Which links DGE uses is a good question for your project team.WordJKM (Japan Korea Marker): a daily LNG price published by S&P Global Platts for cargoes delivered to Japan, South Korea, China and Taiwan [3].

Rule of thumb

$1 per MMBtu ≈ $3.4 million per cargo. A standard cargo holds about 3.4 million MMBtu, so every $1 change in the spread changes the money on that cargo by about $3.4 million.

Four ways an event reaches the spread

A war, a storm, a strike or a new tariff can all hurt DGE. But each one reaches DGE’s money through one of only four doors. In this course we call them impact channels.

ChannelWhat changesWhere on the chain
PriceThe sale price falls or the gas cost rises, so the spread shrinks.Both ends: gas field and buyer
VolumeThere are fewer cargoes to sell, or fewer buyers want them.Gas field, plant, buyer
ShippingThe voyage becomes longer, slower or more expensive, or a route closes.Ship
CreditThe other side of a deal does not pay, or does not do what the contract says.Buyer

The idea to keep

Events are causes. Channels are effects. Your system can tag each event with the channel it hits and the stop where it hits. “Shipping, at the voyage” tells a trader far more than “geopolitics”.

This four-channel model is a simplification made for this course. Risk teams use other names: market risk for our price channel, operational risk for many volume and shipping events, and counterparty credit risk for our credit channel.

For your project

  • Watch both ends of the spread. A jump in the US gas price and a fall in the sale price hurt DGE in the same way.
  • Tag events by channel and stop, not only by topic.
  • Size alerts in money. $1 per MMBtu is about $3.4 million per cargo; multiply by the number of cargoes the event touches.

Questions to ask DGE

  • Which plants supply your cargoes today, and about how many cargoes a month?
  • Which prices are your sales linked to: JKM, oil or Henry Hub? Which of your costs are fixed?
  • Which events hurt your spread most in the last two years?

Lesson 1 · How DGE makes money

Follow One Cargo

By the end of this lesson you can explain how DGE earns money on one shipload of LNG, and say which part of that money a news headline puts at risk.

DGE in one sentence

Diamond Global Energy (DGE) is a Singapore company fully owned by Mitsubishi Corporation (MC). In MC’s own words, DGE “is engaged in the marketing of LNG produced by Mitsubishi Corporation-affiliated projects in the United States and Canada, as well as LNG sales optimization and short-term trading.”SourceMitsubishi Corporation, Energy & Power Solution Group page [4].Old nameUntil 15 March 2026, DGE was called Diamond Gas International (DGI). News before then uses the old name [5].

That sentence gives DGE three jobs:

  • Marketing: finding buyers for MC’s LNG and signing sales deals with them.
  • Sales optimization: choosing the best buyer, port and timing for each cargo.
  • Short-term trading: buying and selling extra cargoes around that supply.

First, what LNG is

Natural gas is hard to carry across an ocean. So it is cooled to −162 °C (−260 °F). At that temperature it turns into a liquid that takes up 600 times less space. This liquid is LNG, liquefied natural gas. Special ships carry it in super-cold tanks. At the other end, an import terminal warms it back into gas.SourceUS Energy Information Administration (EIA), “Liquefied natural gas explained” [1].

One cargo’s journey

  1. Gas field. Gas comes out of wells in the US or Canada and travels by pipeline to the plant.
  2. LNG plant. The plant turns gas into LNG. MC has the right to take LNG from two plants: about 4 million tonnes a year from Cameron LNG in Louisiana (US), and about 2.1 million tonnes a year, its 15% share, from LNG Canada in British Columbia, which shipped its first cargo in June 2025. This right to take a plant’s LNG is called offtake. Together it is roughly 85 cargoes a year, about 7 a month.SourceMC’s group page (Cameron: “approximately 4 million tons of LNG annually”) [4] and MC’s release on LNG Canada’s first cargo, 1 July 2025 [6]. Cargo count: 6.1 million tonnes × about 14 cargoes per million tonnes, our estimate.
  3. LNG ship. One shipload is one cargo. A standard cargo holds about 3.4 million MMBtu of energy. DGE’s public long-term deals deliver the LNG to the buyer’s port, so DGE arranges the ship.WordMMBtu: one million British thermal units, a unit of energy. Gas and LNG prices are quoted in US dollars per MMBtu.SourceThe JKM price standard uses cargoes of 3.3–3.5 million MMBtu [3]. Delivery terms: buyers’ press releases, listed in DGE at a Glance.
  4. Import terminal. The ship unloads in the buyer’s country, and the LNG is warmed back into gas.
  5. Buyer. A power company, a city gas company or another trader uses or resells the gas, and pays for the cargo. DGE’s known long-term buyers are Japanese: JERA, Tokyo Gas, Toho Gas and Tohoku Electric.

How DGE makes money: the spread

For each cargo, the money that stays is the sale price minus all the costs of getting the LNG to the buyer. In this course we call it the spread.

sale pricewhat the buyer pays for the cargo
−gas costUS plants: about 115% of the Henry Hub price
−plant feea fixed fee for turning gas into LNG and loading it
−ship costpaid by the seller when it delivers the cargo
=spreadUS dollars per MMBtu

For US plants, a common estimate of the gas cost is 115% of the Henry Hub price. The extra 15% pays for the fuel the plant burns, pipeline losses and on-site power. On top of that, the company taking the LNG pays a fee for liquefaction, storage and loading that rises with inflation.WordHenry Hub: the main US natural gas price, set at a pipeline hub in Louisiana.SourceEIA, “Ten years after first Sabine Pass cargo”, 24 February 2026 [2].

The sale price depends on the contract. It can follow JKM, the spot price for LNG delivered to Japan, South Korea, China and Taiwan. It can follow the price of oil: many long-term contracts set the LNG price at 12–13% of the Brent oil price. Some contracts even follow Henry Hub, which passes the price risk to the buyer. Which links DGE uses is a good question for your project team.WordJKM (Japan Korea Marker): a daily LNG price published by S&P Global Platts [3].

Rule of thumb

$1 per MMBtu ≈ $3.4 million per cargo. A standard cargo holds about 3.4 million MMBtu, so every $1 change in the spread changes the money on that cargo by about $3.4 million.

Real numbers: a spot cargo in April–June 2026

sale price≈ $17.50: Asian spot LNG, average for April–June 2026 [7]
−gas cost≈ $3.40: 115% of the Henry Hub average of $2.95 [7]
−plant fee≈ $3.00: a typical US fee [8][9]
=before the ship≈ $11 per MMBtu

A long voyage around Africa costs a few dollars per MMBtu. Even so, a cargo sold at the spot price kept a spread of many dollars per MMBtu: tens of millions of dollars on one cargo. Cargoes on long-term contracts earned less, because a formula sets their price: oil-linked LNG cost about $11–13 in the same months.Why so highA war in the Middle East closed the Strait of Hormuz at the end of February 2026. Almost 20% of the world’s LNG had passed through it [7].

Why this will not last

The plant fee is fixed: it is paid whether or not the LNG is taken [8]. The gas cost follows Henry Hub. Only the sale price swings widely. In March 2026, forward prices put Asian LNG at about $9.08 for 2029, while a new US contract cost $7.39–7.77 before shipping [9]. After paying for the ship, little or nothing would be left. Lesson 2 looks at this pressure and the others.

Four ways an event reaches the spread

A war, a storm, a strike or a new tariff can all change DGE’s money. But each one reaches it through one of only four doors. In this course we call them impact channels.

ChannelWhat changesWhere on the chain
PriceThe sale price or the gas cost moves, so the spread changes.Both ends: gas field and buyer
VolumeThere are fewer cargoes to sell, or fewer buyers want them.Gas field, plant, buyer
ShippingThe voyage becomes longer, slower or more expensive, or a route closes.Ship
CreditThe other side of a deal does not pay, or does not do what the contract says.Buyer

The idea to keep

Events are causes. Channels are effects. Your system can tag each event with the channel it hits and the stop where it hits. “Shipping, at the voyage” tells a trader far more than “geopolitics”. Record the direction too: the Hormuz crisis hurt many buyers, but it raised the price that a seller with free cargoes could get.

This four-channel model is a simplification made for this course. Risk teams use other names: market risk for our price channel, operational risk for many volume and shipping events, and counterparty credit risk for our credit channel.

For your project

  • Watch both ends of the spread. A jump in Henry Hub and a fall in Asian prices hurt DGE in the same way.
  • Tag events by channel, stop and direction, not only by topic.
  • Size alerts in money. $1 per MMBtu is about $3.4 million per cargo; multiply by the number of cargoes the event touches.
  • Search old names too. News before March 2026 says “Diamond Gas International” or “DGI”. DGE at a Glance lists the names to filter on.

Questions to ask DGE

  • Which plants supply your cargoes today, and how many cargoes a month?
  • Which prices are your sales linked to: JKM, oil or Henry Hub? Which of your costs are fixed?
  • Which events hurt or helped your spread most in the last two years?

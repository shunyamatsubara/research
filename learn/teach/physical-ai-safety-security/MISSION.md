# Mission: Safety and Security of Physical AI

## Why
Clients are starting physical AI projects: robots whose behavior comes partly from AI models (VLMs, VLAs). I want to be the person who can propose, review, and test the safety and security of these systems, bringing my secure-software skills into the physical world, and talk credibly with robotics safety experts.

## Success looks like
- Given a client's AI-driven robot cell (running example: a robot arm doing pick-and-place with a VLA grasp policy), I can sketch its safety and security layers and say which layer stops which failure, and which failures nothing stops yet.
- I can threat-model a physical AI system (sensors to motors, plus network, cloud, updates, and supply chain) and propose prioritized controls.
- I can name the standards and rules that apply to an AI-driven arm cell (international, EU, Japan), say what each asks for at a high level, and know when to bring in an accredited functional-safety expert.
- I can read a vendor's or startup's safety claims (for example NVIDIA Halos) and ask the questions that separate engineering from marketing.
- I can outline a minimal safety case (claims, arguments, evidence) for a client's system.

## Constraints
- Plain English with simple words; English is not my first language. Short lessons.
- Clients are mainly in Japan, so Japanese rules matter alongside international standards.
- Background: secure software development and AI governance (layered defenses for AI). New to physical AI.

## Out of scope
- Building or training my own robot system (not chosen as a goal on 2026-10-15).
- Deep control-theory math, such as proofs for safety filters (proposed; confirm with the learner).

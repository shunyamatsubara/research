Lesson 8 About 30 minutes · Capstone

Capstone: Assess a Client Cell

A fictional client wants to start an AI pick-and-place project next month. Read the brief, sort the findings, and write the top five for their steering committee. Everything you need is in Lessons 1 to 7.

Your goal

Run a short safety and security assessment of a physical AI project: find the issues, decide when each must be fixed, and explain each one to the client, including whether it is an extension of what they already do or something new.

The brief

Fictional This client and vendor are invented for practice. Any similarity to a real company is not intended.

Client: a mid-sized electronics maker in Japan. Project: an AI cell that sorts connectors into trays at a packing station.

  • Robot. A 6-axis arm with a 400 W largest motor and a 22 kg manipulator, sold by its maker as a "cobot" with built-in power and force limiting. Two packing workers stand about two meters from it.
  • AI. A startup supplies a VLA for the grasp, fine-tuned from an open model on a public model hub, and an LLM planner that takes instructions typed on the line leader's tablet.
  • Compute. The robot PC sends camera images over gRPC to the startup's GPU server in a public cloud and gets actions back. Startup staff can log in remotely for support, including into the safety controller's settings tool. Telemetry is uploaded every five minutes.
  • Data. Two skilled workers will teleoperate the arm for two weeks to record 300 demonstrations. The startup's contract says it may use "all data" to improve its products.
  • Cameras. Two cameras see the work area, including the workers' faces and an unreleased product.
  • Safety claims. The startup says: "Our camera AI detects people with 98% accuracy, so no safety scanner is needed." The grasp has worked in 20 of 20 demo runs, which the startup calls "production-ready".
  • Plan. The PoC runs directly on the production line next month, with no fence. Production is planned in six months. The startup will retrain the VLA every month. The client also exports cells to the EU. There is no SBOM for the AI parts, and near misses on the line are not recorded.

Step 2: four judgment calls

Step 3: write it up

Write the top five findings for the client's steering committee. For each one, give what you found, the layer (L1–L5) or entry point (E1–E8), whether the fix is an extension of what they do today or something new, and the fix.

1. No certified protection for people. The startup offers AI person-detection instead of a safety scanner. L4. Extension: machine safety the client knows, needed for a new reason. Fix: a safety-rated scanner with speed and separation monitoring; keep the AI detection only as an extra layer.

2. The PoC breaks the robot rules. A 400 W arm on the production line with no fence and no recorded risk assessment. Japan's Article 150-4; a trial at a production site is not R&D. Extension. Fix: a separated PoC cell, or a recorded risk assessment that shows no danger.

3. The cloud can reach the safety settings. E8 behind E7. New asset, old controls. Fix: remove remote write access; safety settings change only locally, by an authorized person, with a checksum and a log.

4. The cloud inference link is unknown. E4. Extension. Fix: mutual TLS, no pickle, an allowlisted connection; consider running inference on site.

5. Data, know-how and video. The contract allows "all data"; the cameras record faces and an unreleased product. New asset types. Fix: a data agreement on ownership and reuse before recording; tell the workers, limit purpose and retention; keep the product out of view or out of uploads.

Also worth listing: the 20-run "production-ready" claim (plan statistical pilot trials), no SBOM for AI parts, the unlimited LLM planner (task allowlist), monthly retraining (change process), and no near-miss records.

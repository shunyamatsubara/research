Lesson 6 About 20 minutes · Running example: an AI pick-and-place cell

Build It In from PoC to Production

Most physical AI projects start as a quick PoC. The safety and security work skipped there is hard to add later, and in Japan the PoC itself may already fall under the robot rules. This lesson gives you a stage-by-stage plan: what to build in at each stage, and what evidence each gate needs.

Your goal

For a client's physical AI project, list the minimum safety and security work and the gate evidence for each stage, and explain why test results alone are not enough to go to production.

Warm-up: earlier lessons from memory

Why the PoC matters

Shortcuts taken in a PoC tend to stay: the network design, the data deal with the vendor, the way the AI talks to the robot. Two facts make the PoC more important than it looks.

  • In Japan, a PoC on a client's line is not "R&D". When "完成された機械を生産現場において試行的に使用する" (a completed machine is used on a trial basis at a production site), it does not count as a machine under research and development. If its largest motor is above 80 W, the industrial-robot rules apply, including fences or a recorded risk assessment.MHLW notice 基発第339号, 第3 Ⅰ 1(6).
  • Good startups stage it. Agility Robotics first validates a use case at its own site, then runs on-site validation: "We cordon off a workflow site to separate the pilot phase from your official manufacturing line or live floor." Its humanoid "stays in a sectioned-off workcell", and these workcells "have passed OSHA-recognized Nationally Recognized Test Lab (NRTL) field inspections". Fence-free work is planned for 2027, not assumed from day one.Agility Robotics, "Agility's humanoid deployment process" (4 August 2026). Company blog.

Key rule

Retraining is a change, even when no code changes.

The public draft of UL 4600 requires a safety case analysis "in response to changes", and its examples include "use of a retrained neural network" and new versions of "neural network training tools, training data collection items".UL 4600 voting draft (2019), 12.6.1. The published editions are paywalled. EU law also keeps the trail: the tracing log of safety software uploads is kept "for five years after such upload".EU Machinery Regulation, Annex III, 1.2.1.

Why test results are not enough

A demo that works ten times proves very little. If a policy succeeds in every trial, this is the most you can claim with 95% confidence:

Formula n = ln(1 − C) / ln(1 − p), as used by RAND (2016); the arithmetic is ours. It assumes the trials are independent and match real use.

Three more warnings from the field:

  • Toyota Research Institute evaluated each task "with 50 rollouts per task per policy" and warns that "robotics papers are measuring statistical noise due to insufficient statistical power".TRI LBM Team, "A Careful Examination of Large Behavior Models" (2025, preprint).
  • NVIDIA: "A raw failure rate is not, on its own, a safety argument." And a simulator's "realism must itself be characterized before its outputs can be trusted as safety evidence."NVIDIA Halos Safety Evaluation Framework (2026). Written for cars; the logic applies to robots.
  • Accidents can come from "unsafe interactions of system components, none of which may have failed". A test of each part can pass while the system still causes harm.Leveson & Thomas, STPA Handbook (MIT, 2018).

So tests are one kind of evidence. A production decision also needs hazard analysis, an architecture where L4 holds when the AI fails (Lesson 1), and a safety case that connects them.

A safety case in one page

A safety case is a structured argument that the system is safe enough for a stated use, backed by evidence. The University of York's SACE method walks through eight stages: operating context, hazardous scenarios, safe operating concept, safety requirements, design, hazardous failures, out-of-context operation, and verification.SACE v1.1 (University of York, 2026). A minimal case for the running example:

  • Claim: the cell is acceptably safe for its task under its written operating conditions.
    • Normal use: the grasp-hazard rate is below the target. Evidence: N failure-free trials and a 95% bound.
    • Wrong or hacked AI output cannot cause harm, because L4 limits override it. Evidence: fault-injection and hardware-in-the-loop reports.
    • Unusual input is detected and leads to a safe stop. Evidence: monitor tests with new objects and lighting.
    • Foreseeable misuse and attacks are handled. Evidence: threat model and pentest (Lesson 2).
    • Safety is kept in operation. Evidence: change process; every retraining triggers an impact analysis.

Practice: which gate needs it?

Check yourself

A client wants to move their pick-and-place cell from pilot to production next month. Write the five pieces of evidence you would ask for at that gate.

1. Trial results from the pilot, with failure definitions, trial counts and confidence bounds, under the written operating conditions.

2. Fault-injection results showing that the L4 safety functions work.

3. A penetration test report, with findings fixed or accepted by a named person.

4. A safety case reviewed by someone independent, and the conformity documents (technical file and declaration of conformity; CE marking for the EU).

5. An SBOM that includes models and runtimes, and a change process in which every retraining triggers an impact analysis.

Read the overview of the SACE process and Stage 1 (operating context). About 30 minutes.

Why this one: it is a free, step-by-step method for building the safety case of an autonomous system, with examples. It turns "is it safe?" into a list of claims and evidence you can plan into each project stage.

Lesson 5 About 25 minutes · Running example: an AI pick-and-place cell

Camera Data, Remote Operation and the Cloud

The cell's cameras film workers as well as parts. Operators may run the robot from far away. Data and vendor access cross the factory's boundary. This lesson covers the rules for camera data in Japan and the EU, what keeps remote operation safe when the link fails, and how to control remote access and data sent to the cloud.

Your goal

For an AI robot cell, map every camera, remote connection and outbound data flow. For each one, write its purpose, the rule that applies, and the control, including what happens when the link drops.

Warm-up: Lessons 2 to 4 from memory

The camera has more than one job

In a conventional cell, a camera usually has one job, such as inspection or security. In the running example, the same cameras let the VLA see the parts, record demonstrations and new training data, and may also feed safety monitoring. They also film the operators and anyone who walks past. Each job is a purpose, and privacy law is built on purpose.

Key rule

Write a specific purpose for every camera, including AI training.

Workers cannot guess that a picking camera also records training data. Tell them. In the EU, a purpose of only "safety" or "for your safety" is not specific enough.EDPB, Guidelines 3/2019 on video devices (v2.0, January 2020), para. 15.

Camera data in Japan

When video is personal information

The Personal Information Protection Commission (PPC) says that if a camera captures images that can identify a person, you are handling personal information. You must "利用目的をできる限り特定し" (specify the purpose as far as possible), use the images only for that purpose, and notify or publish the purpose.PPC, ガイドラインに関するＱ＆Ａ (updated 1 July 2025), Q1-13. If a system uses face recognition, the purpose must say so: "顔識別機能を用いていることも明らかにして" (Q1-14).

Video becomes 個人データ (personal data in a database) only when it is organized so that you can search for a specific person. Video you can search only by date and time is not (Q1-41). This matters because duties such as security measures, third-party provision and transfers abroad attach to personal data. A training dataset that is labelled by operator would usually be searchable by person.

Why AI cameras need more explanation

  • The "obvious purpose" exception may not fit. A PPC expert panel warned that as cameras with purposes other than crime prevention spread, even ordinary security cameras might no longer count as "取得の状況からみて利用目的が明らか" (purpose obvious from the situation).PPC, 犯罪予防や安全確保のためのカメラ画像利用に関する有識者検討会 報告書 (March 2023), footnote 56. A camera that records training data has a purpose nobody can guess.
  • Deleting at once still counts. The government's camera guidebook says that even if images are deleted immediately at the edge or in the cloud, it is still "個人情報の取得に当たる" (acquisition of personal information). If images are used as training data for AI retraining, it recommends saying so.IoT Acceleration Consortium, MIC and METI, カメラ画像利活用ガイドブック ver3.0 (March 2022).
  • Workers are not customers. The same guidebook asks whether the employer is "優越的地位を利用して強制をしていないか" (using its stronger position to force it) and says to talk with the workers, their representatives or the union.
  • Monitoring rules apply. For monitoring employees, the PPC says to specify the purpose in advance, put it in internal rules and tell the staff; to name a responsible person; and ideally to notify and consult the union first (Q5-7).

Vendors and clouds

  • A vendor that processes your data for you (委託先) may use it to improve its own analysis technology only "委託元の利用目的の達成に必要な範囲内である限りにおいて" (only as far as your purpose needs) (Q7-39). Statistics it makes outside the entrusted work it "自社のために用いることはできません" (cannot use for itself) (Q7-38).
  • Transfers abroad. Giving personal data to a third party in a foreign country needs the person's prior consent, with information about that country's rules, unless an exception applies: an approved country, or a recipient with an equivalent system.個人情報保護法, Art. 28. If a foreign company running the cloud handles the data, this applies "サーバが国内にある場合であっても" (even if the server is in Japan) (Q12-4). If the provider does not handle the data, set by contract and "適切にアクセス制御を行っている場合等" (with proper access control), it is not a provision (Q7-53).
  • Cloud AI services. Before entering personal data into a generative-AI service, the PPC says to confirm that the provider will not use it for machine learning ("機械学習に利用しないこと等を十分に確認すること").PPC, 生成AIサービスの利用に関する注意喚起等 (2 June 2023). Our inference: the same check applies to camera frames sent to a cloud vision-language model.

A change is coming

An amendment to the Personal Information Protection Act passed on 10 July 2026 and was promulgated on 17 July 2026. In principle it takes effect within two years.PPC, 令和８年改正個人情報保護法について; overview (July 2026). Three parts matter here:

  • A new category, 特定生体個人情報, for biometric data that can be collected without special technology or high cost, such as face features, with a duty to tell people certain things in advance (new Art. 21-2). The Diet asked the PPC to make sure notices are posted clearly "監視カメラやセンサー等の機器の周辺に" (around cameras and sensors).
  • Third-party provision without consent when data is used only to make statistics, "統計作成等であると整理できるAI開発等を含む" (including AI development that counts as making statistics).
  • Entrusted vendors must not handle data "委託を受けた業務の遂行に必要な範囲を超えて" (beyond what the entrusted work needs).

The detailed rules are still being drafted. Check their status on every project.

If the cell is in the EU

  • Video baseline. "An employee in his/her workplace is in most cases not likely expecting to be monitored by his or her employer." Video should usually be erased "after a few days"; keeping it beyond 72 hours needs more justification. And: "Functions provided, but not necessary, must be deactivated."EDPB Guidelines 3/2019, paras. 37, 121 and 129.
  • Emotion recognition at work is banned. The AI Act prohibits AI systems that "infer emotions of a natural person in the areas of workplace and education institutions", except for medical or safety reasons. This has applied since 2 February 2025.AI Act (EU) 2024/1689, Art. 5(1)(f) and Art. 113. The Commission reads "workplace" to include "factories and warehouses", and the safety exception as applying "only in relation to the protection of life and health".European Commission, Guidelines on prohibited AI practices, C(2025) 5052 (29 July 2025), non-binding. Pain and fatigue are not emotions under the Act: "It does not include physical states, such as pain or fatigue" (Recital 18). The point for physical AI: the ban depends on what the model infers, not on the camera. A vision-language model in the cell could cross the line with the same frames.
  • The factory gets rights to the robot's data. For connected products placed on the market after 12 September 2026, product data must be available to the user "by default, easily, securely, free of charge". The maker may use non-personal data only "on the basis of a contract with the user", and may not use it "to derive insights about the economic situation, assets and production methods of" the user.Data Act (EU) 2023/2854, Art. 3(1), Art. 4(13) and Art. 50. Recital 14 names "industrial machinery". This is EU law, but it is a useful model for contracts in Japan too (Lesson 4).

Remote operation: safe when the link fails

Japan's new way of thinking

A Japanese government expert panel on machines that run without an operator on board set out two terms in June 2026:MHLW, 機械の無人運転における安全確保等に関する専門家検討会 中間とりまとめ (29 June 2026).

  • 自律運転 (autonomous operation): in normal operation the machine is in control, and the human role is limited to starting or restarting, monitoring and emergency stop.
  • 遠隔運転 (remote operation): in normal operation a human is in control, operating from a distance.

The panel says modes should switch "人による明示的な操作など明確な条件と手順で" (by clear conditions and procedures, such as an explicit human action). For machines covered by Japan's safety law, one remote operator should run only one machine at a time: "一人が同時に運転可能な機械は１台のみ". The measures in the panel's summary include stopping the machine automatically when communication fails, for example a lost link or a long delay.MHLW, 中間とりまとめ（概要） (June 2026). The panel also says that machines using "フィジカルＡＩやＡＩロボティクス" that no specific rule covers yet will be examined with the same approach.

What the standards and EU law say

  • EU Machinery Regulation: "For wireless control, a failure of the communication or connection or a faulty connection shall not lead to a hazardous situation." The interruption or re-establishment of a "communication network connection" must not lead to hazardous situations either.EU Machinery Regulation 2023/1230, Annex III, 1.2.1 and 1.2.6.
  • IEC 60204-1 (cableless control): when the control signal has been lost for a time set by risk assessment, "an automatic stop of the machine shall be initiated". And: "Restoration of the ability of a CCS to control a machine shall not restart the machine."IEC 60204-1:2016, 9.2.4, as quoted in an IDEC seminar deck for the Japan Machinery Federation (November 2016). The standard itself is paywalled.

A real lesson from a humanoid maker

Agility Robotics first put the safety controller for its Digit humanoid outside the robot. "If there were communication issues with the robot it would fall." It then added a safety PLC on the robot and "category 1 stop functionality so that Digit could come to a controlled stop before power fully cut or if communication with the robot was lost". Even so, each Digit deployed in the field "operates behind a set of physical safety barriers".Agility Robotics, "Built for the real world" (22 June 2026). A company blog: first-hand, but promotional.

Key rule

The safe state must not depend on the network.

When the link drops, the robot stops by itself, with safety functions that run on the robot or in the cell. When the link returns, nothing restarts until a person decides.

You already know this

Fail closed. A lost connection leads to a safe state, and a restored one does not resume old work by itself, just as a dropped session must log in again. The difference here: "safe" means the arm stops moving, and it must be designed so it stops without falling or dropping a load.

Teleoperation to record demonstrations

In Japan, 教示等 (teaching) means setting, changing or checking the manipulator's motion order, position or speed within its motion range. It requires special education, and Art. 150-3 requires measures to stop the robot at once in an abnormal situation.Ordinance on Industrial Safety and Health, Art. 36(31) and Art. 150-3. Whether teleoperation to record demonstrations counts as teaching is not settled: we found no official interpretation. Until it is, apply the teaching rules, and ask the local Labour Standards Inspection Office.

The operator is also a data source. A study of virtual-reality users found that a person "can be uniquely identified amongst the entire pool of 50,000+ with 94.33% accuracy from 100 seconds of motion"."Unique Identification of 50,000+ Virtual Reality Users from Head & Hand Motion Data" (2023). Our inference: motion recorded with a VR teleoperation rig can identify the operator. Treat teleoperation recordings as personal data.

Vendor remote access

Vendor support is entry point E7. Government guidance for operational technology (OT) is clear and recent:

  • "The authoring agencies strongly recommend a jump host for adding user authentication to legacy networks and enforcing segmentation." "Log all privileged sessions for audit and forensic purposes." Give just-in-time access for maintenance windows.CISA and partners, Adapting Zero Trust Principles to Operational Technology (29 April 2026).
  • "All connections with the OT environment should be initiated as outbound connections from within the OT environment." Break-glass access "should not be used as a standard remote access method".UK NCSC, Secure connectivity principles for OT, Principles 2 and 7 (January 2026).
  • "Remote access should not circumvent or negate safety or security controls." Make sure "operations personnel are aware of planned remote activity".NIST SP 800-82r3 (September 2023).
  • Japan's factory security guideline asks for two-factor authentication, limits on which devices each remote user can reach, limits on connection times, and detection of connections outside maintenance periods.METI, 工場システムにおけるサイバー・フィジカル・セキュリティ対策ガイドライン Ver1.1 (11 April 2025), checklist item 3-6.

The physical AI twist: a vendor's remote path may reach the policy server, the training pipeline and the cameras, not just a controller. Keep the safety settings off the remote path entirely, and make safety changes on site, by an authorized person (Lesson 3).

Data to the cloud

The best single guide here comes from CISA and agencies in six other countries:CISA and partners, Principles for the Secure Integration of Artificial Intelligence in Operational Technology (3 December 2025).

  • "Encourage push-based architectures where data is pushed out of the OT network for AI systems to use without persistent access into the OT network."
  • "Operators may not want vendors to train AI on operational data as it could include IP or be sensitive."
  • "Ask if vendors can operate on premises or without continuous access to the internet/vendor cloud."
  • Find out how the vendor will store and use the data, "especially if it involves remote, cloud, or offshore access".

Robots can send more than you expect. A security analysis of the Unitree G1 humanoid reports that it sends sensor and service-state telemetry to outside servers "every 300 seconds without operator notice"."Cybersecurity AI: Humanoid Robots as Attack Vectors" (2025, preprint by authors from a robot-security company). Ask every vendor for a list of what is sent, where and how often, and check it with a network capture. From 11 December 2027, the EU Cyber Resilience Act requires products to "process only data, personal or other, that are adequate, relevant and limited to what is necessary in relation to the intended purpose".Cyber Resilience Act, Annex I, Part I(2)(g), and Art. 71.

Practice: name the area

Check yourself

For the running example, write the cameras' purposes and how workers are told. Then write what must happen when the remote link drops, and when it comes back.

Purposes. Perception for the VLA; recording demonstrations and new training data; safety monitoring if used. Each one is written down, shown on signs at the cell, put in internal rules, and discussed with the workers or their union. Face recognition, if any, is named.

Link drops. After a risk-assessed time the robot stops by itself, using safety functions on the robot or in the cell that do not need the network. No silent switch to autonomous mode.

Link returns. Nothing restarts by itself. A person restarts it with a deliberate action, and the robot shows its current mode.

For client work

  • What does each camera record: faces, products, documents? For which purposes, for how long, and who can see it? How were workers told?
  • Are recordings searchable by person (personal data)? Who outside the company handles them, and in which country?
  • Does any cloud service or vendor use the video or telemetry to train its own models?
  • Who can operate the robot remotely? What happens when the link drops, and when it returns? Has that been tested?
  • How do vendors connect: jump host, MFA, time windows, recorded sessions, outbound-only? Can a remote session reach the safety settings?
  • What does the robot send outside, where and how often? Has anyone checked with a network capture?

Read the primary source

CISA and partners, "Principles for the Secure Integration of Artificial Intelligence in Operational Technology" (December 2025)

25 pages, in English. About 40 minutes.

Why this one: agencies from seven countries wrote it for exactly this case, AI inside industrial control. It covers push-based design, vendor data use, on-premises options and fail-safe practices. For the Japanese privacy side, then read PPC Q&A items Q1-13, Q1-14, Q5-7, Q7-38, Q7-39 and Q12-4, and the MHLW summary on remote operation.

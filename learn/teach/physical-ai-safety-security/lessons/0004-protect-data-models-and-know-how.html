Lesson 4 About 25 minutes · Running example: an AI pick-and-place cell

Protect Data, Models and Know-How

In a physical AI project, a factory's skill moves into files: recorded demonstrations, datasets and trained models. This lesson shows how those files can be lost, copied or changed, what Japanese law and contracts protect, and how to manage models, datasets and open-source parts as a software supply chain.

Your goal

For an AI robot cell, list the assets that hold know-how. For each one, say how it could leak or be changed, and write three kinds of controls: secrecy measures, contract terms, and an inventory with signatures and change control.

Warm-up: Lessons 1 to 3 from memory

Where the know-how goes

In a conventional cell, the know-how sits in a robot program and in the heads of skilled workers. In a physical AI cell, it moves into data. In the running example, the client's best operator records 300 grasps by teleoperation, and a VLA is fine-tuned on them. From that day, the operator's skill also exists as a dataset and as a set of model weights.

AssetIn the running exampleWhy it matters
Demonstrations300 teleoperated grasps, with camera images and arm motionsThe operator's tacit skill, in a form a machine can learn from
Process dataGrip forces, approach angles, cycle times, reject ratesThe process recipe and the quality level
Camera imagesParts, fixtures, the line layout, sometimes products not yet releasedProduct and layout secrets. Often personal data too (Lesson 5)
Fine-tuned modelVLA weights trained on the demonstrationsThe skill in compressed form. It can be copied and run elsewhere
ConfigurationAllowlists, motion limits, safety settingsHow the cell is protected. A target for tampering (Lesson 3)

Two Japanese government guides point the same way. METI's trade-secret guideline says that intangible know-how that employees have learned, such as skills, must in principle be made visible "紙その他の媒体に" (on paper or another medium) to be protected, for example as a list of categories or a written document.METI, 営業秘密管理指針 (revised March 2025), section on intangible know-how. And METI's AI contract guideline notes that for a robot with delicate movements, the robot's hardware strongly shapes the training data, so "ユーザがノウハウを有しているような場合もある" (the user may be the one who holds the know-how).METI, AI・データの利用に関する契約ガイドライン（AI編） (June 2018), section on know-how.

Our inference from these two points: a demonstration dataset may be the first time a factory's tacit skill exists on a medium at all. That makes it easier to protect, and also easier to steal.

Key rule

The demonstrations are the know-how.

Protect the dataset and the model trained on it as you would protect the skill itself. Decide who owns them, who may use them, and how they are kept secret before the first recording.

Five ways to lose it

WayWhat happensFirst control
1. StolenSomeone copies the dataset or the weights from storage, the policy server or a cloud bucket (entry points E3, E4, E7).Access control, encryption, logging
2. Published by defaultAn open-source tool uploads the data to a public site because nobody changed a default.Change the defaults; review every upload
3. Copied by watchingSomeone builds a working copy of the policy from its behavior, without the files.Limit who can query, watch or record the robot
4. Reused by the vendorThe vendor uses your data or model to improve its own products.Contract terms before recording starts
5. PoisonedSomeone changes the training data so the model learns a hidden behavior.Hashes, change records, review of demonstrations

Published by default

LeRobot, a popular open-source robot-learning library, says: "By default, the dataset is pushed to your Hugging Face page after recording. To disable uploading, use --dataset.push_to_hub=False."LeRobot documentation, "Imitation learning on real-world robots" (checked October 2026). Defaults can change between versions. On the Hugging Face Hub, a new repository is public unless you say otherwise: "If None (default), the repo will be public unless the organization's default is private."huggingface_hub API reference, create_repo. In a client project, set the upload flags off, make the organization's default private, and check every upload. This is the same mistake as a public storage bucket, made by a tool on someone's behalf.

Copied by watching

You do not need the files to copy a policy. A study on stealing reinforcement-learning models found that "the process of DRL model extraction is equivalent to imitation learning": record what the policy does, and train a copy on it."Stealing Deep Reinforcement Learning Models for Fun and Profit" (2020). Another, at ICML 2024, starts from the point that control policies "represent valuable intellectual property", and steals them with only black-box access."Stealthy Imitation: Reward-guided Environment-free Policy Stealing" (ICML 2024). Even encrypted network traffic can show which action a collaborative robot is running."On the Feasibility of Fingerprinting Collaborative Robot Network Traffic" (2023, preprint). And a 2026 preprint presents the first study of membership inference against VLA models: guessing whether a given demonstration was in the training data."Membership Inference Attacks on Vision-Language-Action Models" (2026, preprint).

NIST's view of the limits: exact copies are not possible, but "a functionally equivalent model can be reconstructed", and differential privacy "does not provide guarantees against model extraction attacks".NIST AI 100-2 E2025, Adversarial Machine Learning (March 2025), section on model extraction. So the controls are about access: keep the policy server inside the cell zone, limit and log queries, and treat motion logs and videos of the robot as secret.

Poisoned

Poisoning is an old idea with a physical twist. A 2025 preprint shows that "attackers can manipulate VLAs by simply injecting physical objects as triggers into the training dataset"."Goal-oriented Backdoor Attack against Vision-Language-Action Models via Physical Objects" (2025, preprint). An object in the scene during recording can become a hidden switch. Keep hashes of every dataset, record who changed what, and review the demonstrations before training. L3 and L4 still limit what a poisoned model can do to people.

You already know this

Ways 1 and 5 are the classic threats to confidentiality and integrity. Way 2 is a misconfigured default. Ways 3 and 4 are the newer ones: the asset can leak through the robot's behavior and through the model itself, even when no file is stolen.

What Japanese law protects, and where it stops

Trade secrets (営業秘密)

Under the Unfair Competition Prevention Act, a trade secret is information that is "秘密として管理されている" (managed as a secret), useful for business, and "公然と知られていない" (not publicly known). The law's first example of useful information is "生産方法" (production methods).不正競争防止法, Art. 2(6). All three conditions must hold.

METI's guideline sets the "最低限の水準の対策" (minimum level of measures) needed for legal protection. The key test is whether employees can tell that the company intends to keep the information secret. For electronic data, a ㊙ mark in file or folder names, a mark shown when the file opens, or a password can each be enough. Storing secrets in an outside cloud does not by itself lose protection, as long as they are managed as secret, for example with layered access control.METI, 営業秘密管理指針 (revised March 2025), section on electronic information.

The March 2025 revision added notes on AI. Using secret data to train an AI model inside the company does not, by itself, end its protection, even if the model later outputs the data. But if the data goes to a third party outside the company, such as a generative-AI provider, "秘密管理性が否定される場合もあり得る" (its status as a managed secret may be denied).Same guideline, note 2 and footnote 24.

Shared data (限定提供データ)

Data you share with partners can be protected as 限定提供データ: information provided to specific parties as a business and "相当量蓄積され、及び管理されている" (accumulated in substantial quantity and managed by electronic means).不正競争防止法, Art. 2(7). METI's guideline lists training datasets and trained models as examples. But it also says that when a model trained on the data is judged "もはや元の限定提供データとは異なるもの" (no longer the same as the original data), using it is not unfair competition.METI, 限定提供データに関する指針 (revised February 2024). The protection may not follow the data into the model.

Contracts

METI's AI contract guideline starts from a simple point: "データは、無体物（情報）であるため、所有権の対象とはなり得ない" (data is intangible, so it cannot be owned). Rights to data come from contracts, together with trade-secret, copyright and privacy law.METI, 契約ガイドライン（AI編）, section on the legal nature of data.

The same guideline warns that a ban on using a trained model does not by itself ban new models made from it by additional training or distillation. "再利用モデルや蒸留モデルの生成や利用を制限したい場合には、その旨を契約書に明記することが重要となる" (to restrict fine-tuned or distilled models, state it in the contract).

METI's 2025 checklist for AI contracts adds a category that is easy to miss: "インプット処理成果", what the vendor creates from your inputs, including "学習用データ、中間生成物、派生的知的財産" (training data, intermediate products and derived IP). It also warns that "一度AIモデル（学習済みモデル）に取り込まれた情報の除去は一般的には困難" (once information is absorbed into a trained model, removing it is generally difficult). Its basic advice: keep unneeded information out of the inputs.METI, AIの利用・開発に関する契約チェックリスト (February 2025).

Key rule

Data cannot be owned. Keep it secret, and agree in writing who may use it.

Sign the contract before the first recording. Data inside a trained model is hard to take back.

For the running example, the contract should answer these questions:

  • Who may use the 300 demonstrations, for what purpose, and for how long?
  • May the vendor use the data, or the model trained on it, to improve its own products or to serve other customers?
  • Are fine-tuning and distillation of the trained model allowed? By whom?
  • Who owns improvements and derived models?
  • How are data and models returned or deleted at the end, knowing that data inside a model is hard to remove?
  • Which audit rights and logs let the client check all of this?

Manage AI parts as a software supply chain

You already know this

SBOMs, code signing, pinned dependencies and "never deserialize untrusted data" all apply. The difference: a model file is part code, part data. It decides behavior like code, but you cannot read it like code. A signature proves who made it, not how it behaves.

Keep an inventory (AIBOM)

Japan's AI Guidelines for Business, version 1.2, now ask AI developers to create an AIBOM next to the SBOM: "AIシステムの構成管理を容易にするAIBOMを作成" (create an AIBOM to make configuration management of AI systems easier).METI and MIC, AI事業者ガイドライン（第1.2版） (March 2026), appendix, guidance for AI developers. The formats exist. CycloneDX added a machine-learning BOM (ML-BOM) in version 1.5 (June 2023), and SPDX 3.0 has an AI profile with fields such as autonomyType and safetyRiskAssessment.CycloneDX v1.5 announcement (26 June 2023); SPDX 3.0.1, AIPackage. OWASP's advice: "AI BOMs and ML SBOMs are an emerging area and you should evaluate options starting with OWASP CycloneDX."OWASP LLM03:2025 Supply Chain.

For robots, J-AISI's robotics guide expects that "ソフトウェア／モデル／設定内容のバージョンや更新履歴が管理されている" (versions and update history of software, models and settings are managed), and that each change has a defined content, timing, scope of impact and owner for verification.J-AISI, AIロボティクスに関するセーフティ評価観点ガイド v1.0 (July 2026), configuration and change management.

In the EU, the Cyber Resilience Act requires a software bill of materials "covering at the very least the top-level dependencies of the products".Cyber Resilience Act (EU) 2024/2847, Annex I, Part II(1). We found no official text that says whether model weights and datasets must be listed. List them anyway. The Machinery Regulation requires that "software and data that are critical" for meeting its safety requirements "shall be identified as such and shall be adequately protected against accidental or intentional corruption".EU Machinery Regulation 2023/1230, Annex III, 1.1.9. It applies from 20 January 2027.

What to record for each AI part (a suggested minimum):

FieldFor the grasp VLA
IdentityName, version, file hash, signature
OriginSupplier, base model and its license, where it was downloaded from
Training dataDataset IDs and hashes; when, where and by whom it was recorded; the contract or consent that allows the use

Where it runsWhich robots and servers, and how to roll back

This list is our suggestion, built from the sources above. CycloneDX and SPDX can hold these fields.

Sign, and load safely

  • Sign every model release and check the signature before loading. The OpenSSF model-signing tool, "a tool for signing and verifying ML models", reached version 1.0 in April 2025.model-signing on PyPI (1.0.0 released 4 April 2025).
  • Never load untrusted pickle files. Hugging Face warns: "There are dangerous arbitrary code execution attacks that can be perpetrated when you load a pickle file." Use safetensors, a format "for storing tensors safely (as opposed to pickle)".Hugging Face, "Pickle Scanning"; safetensors documentation.
  • Pin and mirror. Pin model and dataset versions as you pin dependencies, and keep approved copies inside the company.

Retraining is a change

A retrained model is a new model. UL 4600 requires a safety case analysis in response to changes, and its examples include "update to training data".UL 4600 voting draft (2019), 12.6.1. The published editions are paywalled. Treat each retraining like a software release: impact analysis, re-validation, approval, and an inventory update (Lesson 6).

Medical devices show one way to plan for this. The US FDA lets makers describe planned model changes in advance, in a "Predetermined Change Control Plan", so that each listed change can be made "without necessitating additional marketing submissions".US FDA, PCCP final guidance for AI-enabled device software (August 2025). The idea transfers to a robot cell: agree in advance which retraining is allowed and how each one is tested.

Practice: name the control

Check yourself

For the running example, name four assets that hold know-how. For each one, write one control, and say whether it keeps the asset secret, keeps it unchanged, or decides who may use it.

Demonstrations. Marked ㊙, access for the project team only, upload to public hubs turned off (secret). A contract on who may use them, for what and how long (rights).

Fine-tuned model. Signed releases checked before loading (unchanged). Contract terms that name fine-tuning and distillation (rights). Queries limited and logged (secret).

Training datasets. A hash and a change record for each one, and a review before training (unchanged).

Camera images and process data. Only what the task needs; kept inside the company or under contract (secret, rights). See Lesson 5 for personal data.

For client work

  • Which data and models carry the client's know-how? Who owns and may use each one under the contract, and was that agreed before recording?
  • Are these assets managed as trade secrets: marked, limited, logged?
  • Does any tool upload data or models by default? To where, public or private?
  • Can the vendor reuse the data or the model? Does the contract name fine-tuning and distillation?
  • Is there an inventory of models, datasets and open-source parts, with hashes, signatures and origins?
  • What happens when a model is retrained: who analyzes the impact, who re-tests, who approves?

Read the primary source

METI, 「AIの利用・開発に関する契約チェックリスト」 (February 2025)

In Japanese. Read the overview and the sections on inputs, outputs and インプット処理成果. About 30 minutes.

Why this one: it is the most practical Japanese government guide to who may use what in an AI contract, and it is recent. Then read the AI notes in the 営業秘密管理指針 (March 2025) for the trade-secret side.

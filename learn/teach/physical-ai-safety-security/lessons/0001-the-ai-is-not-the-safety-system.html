Lesson 1 About 15 minutes · Running example: an AI pick-and-place cell

The AI Is Not the Safety System

In an AI robot cell, the AI model makes the robot useful. A separate, simpler layer keeps people safe. Experts from AI labs, chip makers, regulators and safety research all agree on this. This lesson shows why, and how to tell which layer should stop which failure.

Your goal

Look at a failure in an AI pick-and-place cell, name the layer you should rely on to stop it, and explain to a client why the AI model cannot be that layer.

A wrong answer that moves

In the software you secure today, a model's wrong answer is text on a screen. A person can read it, ignore it, or roll it back. In a pick-and-place cell, the model's answer is a motion command. It moves a steel arm with real speed and force, close to real people. There is no undo.

So the main question changes. In software you ask, "How do we make the model's answer right?" In a robot cell you must also ask, "What stops the arm when the answer is wrong?"

Even good AI cannot be the safety function

Google DeepMind tested leading AI models as a human-safety monitor. Each model looked at a camera image and decided whether a person was too close to a robot. When a model was tuned to rarely stop the robot by mistake (fewer than 5% false alarms), it missed more than 40% of real hazards. Models tuned to miss fewer hazards (10–15%) stopped the robot for no reason 15–25% of the time. No model was good at both. DeepMind's conclusion: use these models "alongside deterministic, low-level safety guardrails".Source: Google DeepMind, Gemini Robotics 2: Safety Evaluations (29 July 2026), p. 5.

You already know this

This is the intrusion-detection trade-off. A detector tuned for few false alarms misses attacks. A detector tuned to catch everything floods you with alerts. In security, you never let one detector be your only control. In a robot cell, you never let an AI model be the only thing that keeps people safe.

The people who build these models say the same thing about their own products:

The reason is simple. A learned model's failures cannot be listed or bounded in advance, so nobody can prove how it behaves in the next new situation. A safety function needs exactly that kind of proof.

The pattern: the AI proposes, a checker decides

Safety engineers handle this with one pattern. A complex part does the work. A simple, independent part watches it and can always force the machine into a safe state. The safety researcher Philip Koopman calls these the doer and the checker.Koopman, Kane & Black, "Credible Autonomy Safety Argumentation" (2019), pp. 6–7. Related designs are also called a monitor/actuator pair, the Simplex architecture, or runtime assurance.

You can find this pattern everywhere in the field:

  • NVIDIA Halos for Robotics: a monitor flags camera input that looks unlike the AI's training data, because then "the accuracy and reliability of the AI model can no longer be assured". A separate Safety Decision Maker then makes the robot "fall back to a safe operating state". It runs on a safety processor "isolated from the main AI compute domain".NVIDIA Technical Blog, "Inside NVIDIA Halos for Robotics" (22 June 2026).
  • Agility Robotics, a humanoid startup, added a safety PLC (a certified safety computer) inside its robot, because "if the humanoid's main control system had a software bug or made a bad decision, the safety PLC was there to step in".Agility Robotics, "Built for the Real World" (22 June 2026).
  • The international technical report on AI and functional safety lists, as one of its main topics, the "use of non-AI safety related functions to ensure safety for an AI controlled equipment".ISO/IEC TR 5469:2024, scope (official preview).
Key rule

The AI may propose any motion. An independent checker decides what the arm is allowed to do.

In the EU this becomes law on 20 January 2027. For a machine whose behavior changes through learning, the control system "shall not cause the machinery or related product to perform actions beyond its defined task and movement space".EU Machinery Regulation 2023/1230, Annex III, section 1.2.1. Application date: European Commission.

You already know this

A reference monitor must be always invoked, tamperproof, and small enough to verify.NIST glossary, "reference monitor" (from NIST SP 800-53 Rev. 5). The comparison is a teaching analogy, not wording from a safety standard. A good safety checker needs the same three properties:

  • Always invoked: it watches every motion, not only the motions the AI thinks are risky.
  • Tamperproof: it runs on separate, safety-rated hardware. Neither the AI nor an attacker who controls the AI computer can change it.
  • Verifiable: it is simple enough to certify to a reliability level, such as a performance level (PL) under ISO 13849-1.

Koopman gives a test that will feel familiar to you. Imagine that the doer "maliciously attempts to compromise safety in the worst way possible, with full and complete knowledge of every aspect of the design. Could it compromise safety?" If the answer is yes, your checker is not independent enough.Koopman, Kane & Black, "Credible Autonomy Safety Argumentation" (Safety-Critical Systems Symposium 2019), p. 8. The security company Exein says the same thing from the security side: "Any mitigation that depends on the model behaving is not a mitigation."Exein, "Threat modeling physical AI" (10 July 2026).

Five layers in a pick-and-place cell

A real cell has more than one check. This course uses five layers that a motion command passes through, from the request down to the moving steel. The numbers follow the path of a command; they are not a ranking. DeepMind describes its safety approach as "a stack of Swiss cheese slices. No single slice is a perfect barrier."Google DeepMind, "Responsibly advancing AI and robotics".

The most important line is the dashed one between L3 and L4. L3 runs in your normal software stack. It is smart and catches most problems early, so the robot can recover smoothly. But it is not certified, and it runs on computers that a bug or an attacker can take over. L4 is certified and independent. When a person can get hurt, you rely on L4. A useful question for any client is: "Is this limit enforced by a safety-rated function, or only by our own code?"

What L4 does

  • Protective stop: a safety function stops the robot in a controlled way, for example when a person comes too close.
  • Speed and separation monitoring: safety-rated sensors, such as laser scanners, measure how close a person is. The robot slows down and stops before contact. The required distance comes from how fast a person can approach, the robot's reaction time, and its stopping distance.ISO 10218-1/-2:2025 include these collaborative modes, which came from ISO/TS 15066. Summary in the DeepMind safety report, p. 12.
  • Power and force limiting: the robot is built so that if it touches a person, the force stays below set limits for each body region.

A safe state is not always "power off". A legged robot falls when its motor power is cut. That is why Boston Dynamics' Spot first sits down and only then cuts power, and why humanoid makers design stops that keep power long enough to stay balanced. Choosing the right kind of stop is part of the design.Boston Dynamics Spot SDK, E-Stop service; IEEE Humanoid Study Group report (September 2025).

In Japan, the law measures the hazard by where the arm can physically reach. It does not use the path the program plans. An official notice from the labour ministry (MHLW) defines the robot's motion range (可動範囲) as the maximum range its parts can structurally move, tool included. Only electrical or mechanical stoppers make it smaller.MHLW notice 基発第339号 (1983, as amended in 2013), section 3 I 1(7). So a VLA that "intends" to stay in a small area does not make the hazard smaller. A safety-rated limit does.

Practice: which layer stops it?

For each failure, choose the layer you should rely on to stop it. That means the layer whose job this is, and which still works when the layers above it are wrong.

Check yourself

A client asks: "Our VLA is 99% accurate. Why do we still need a separate safety controller?" Answer in three sentences, without looking back.

1. Accuracy describes average behavior in tested conditions. The remaining errors, new situations and attacks cannot be bounded in advance, so the model cannot be certified as a safety function, and its makers say so too.

2. Safety needs a checker that still works when the AI is wrong or compromised: a small, independent, safety-rated layer (protective stop, speed and separation monitoring, force and zone limits) that the AI computer cannot change.

3. The AI makes the cell productive and the checker keeps people safe. In the EU, from January 2027, a learning machine must not act beyond its defined task and movement space.

Extension vs New

Your starting idea was that some measures for physical AI extend what a security program already does, and others are new. This page collects the "extension or new" summary from every lesson, organized by your seven themes. Use it to plan an assessment, a proposal or a client briefing.

How to read it. "Extension" means an existing control that still works, with new targets or new settings. "New" means something that needs new thinking, new evidence or new rules. Most themes have both. Each table links to the lesson that explains it.

1. Permissions and motion range of the AI (AIの判断・動作範囲の制御)

From Lesson 1, Lesson 2 and Lesson 3.

Extension of conventional measuresNew for physical AI
Authorization outside the application, least privilege, default-denyBounding a model whose output is continuous motion, not a list of API calls
A separate identity for the AI process, never rootPermissions about meaning (which object goes where), checked outside the model by a guardrail grounded in the scene, because jailbreaks reach the planner (E2)
Change control, keys and logs for configuration (E8)Safety settings treated as the most valuable asset to protect (E8)
Safety-rated limits, fences and lockout from machine safety (ISO 10218; Japan Art. 150-3 to 150-5)A certified safety layer that still holds after a full takeover of the AI computer (Koopman's test)
Training before risky work (special education)EU law: a learning machine must stay within its "defined task and movement space"; machine-learning safety components need third-party assessment; a year of safety-decision data for self-evolving machines
 Collaboration as a property of the application: no AI and no robot model is "safe" on its own

First question for the client: "What can the AI ask the robot to do, and what stops the arm if it asks for something else?"

2. Training data and manufacturing know-how (学習データ・製造ノウハウの保護)

From Lesson 4.

Extension of conventional measuresNew for physical AI
Trade-secret measures (marking, access limits, NDAs), now for datasets and modelsTacit skill becomes data: the demonstrations are the know-how and the main target
Contracts on data useContracts that must name fine-tuning and distillation, because a ban on use does not cover derived models
Data integrity with hashes and recordsCopying by watching: model extraction works like imitation learning, and even encrypted traffic can show which action runs
Access control and encryption for storageData inside a trained model is hard to remove, and legal protection may not follow the data into the model
 Poisoning through physical objects placed in the scene during recording
 EU Data Act: a robot maker may not use the robot's data to learn the user's "production methods" (Lesson 5)

First question for the client: "Which data and models hold your know-how, and who may use each one under the contract?"

3. Configuration management of models and open-source parts (AIモデル・OSS等の構成管理)

From Lesson 2, Lesson 4 and Lesson 6.

Extension of conventional measuresNew for physical AI
SBOM, extended to an AIBOM or ML-BOM (now asked for in Japan's AI Guidelines for Business v1.2)An inventory that also lists models, datasets and inference runtimes; no official rule yet on whether weights belong in the EU's SBOM
Code signing, extended to model signingA signature proves origin, not safe behavior
Never deserialize untrusted data: no pickle, use safetensors (E3, E4)Backdoors in weights that code review cannot see (E3)
Version control and change records for configurationEvery retraining is a change that needs re-validation, with no code change
Vulnerability handling and patching, including the robot middleware (E5)Open-source robot tools that publish data by default

First question for the client: "Can you list every model, dataset and open-source part in the cell, with versions, hashes and origins?"

4. Remote operation, remote access and the cloud (遠隔操作・クラウド接続のセキュリティ)

From Lesson 2 and Lesson 5.

Extension of conventional measuresNew for physical AI
Network zones and conduits around the cell (E4, E5, E7)Motion commands that arrive over the network from a policy server (E4)
OT remote access: jump host, MFA, just-in-time access, recorded sessions, outbound-onlyA vendor's remote path that can reach the policy server, the training pipeline and the cameras, not just a controller
Stop on link loss, and no restart on reconnection (IEC 60204-1; EU Machinery Regulation)Japan's new terms for autonomous and remote operation, with physical AI next in line for review
Mode selectors and one operator per machineTeleoperation turns workers into data sources: video, voice and motion that can identify them
Data minimization (EU Cyber Resilience Act); unique keys per device (E6)Fleet learning: vendors want customer data to train their models, and contracts must limit it
 Robot telemetry to vendor clouds, sometimes without notice

First question for the client: "What happens when the link drops, and who can connect into the cell from outside?"

5. Camera data (カメラデータ等の取り扱い)

From Lesson 2, Lesson 4 and Lesson 5.

Extension of conventional measuresNew for physical AI
CCTV rules: purpose, notice, retention, security (PPC Q&A; EDPB)One camera with several purposes: perception, training data and safety. AI training must be stated
Rules for monitoring employees: purpose in internal rules, tell staff, consult the unionA model can infer emotions from the same frames. In the EU this is banned at work, except for medical or safety reasons
Cloud, entrustment and cross-border rules (APPI Art. 28; PPC Q&A)The scene is an input channel that no firewall sees (E1)
 Frames that train the AI also record products and layouts, so they are trade secrets too
 Japan's 2026 amendment adds rules for biometric data such as face features (in force within two years)

First question for the client: "What does each camera record, for which purposes, and how were the workers told?"

6. Conventional robots vs physical AI (従来ロボットとフィジカルAIのリスクの違い)

From Lesson 1 and Lesson 7.

Extension of conventional measuresNew for physical AI
The hazard is the same moving arm: same safety functions, same Japanese rulesBehavior comes from a model learned from data, so it must be tested statistically
The doer/checker split from safety engineering: the AI is the doerSuccess can fall sharply under small changes, such as a moved camera, so operating conditions must be written down
Old protections for new asset types: data, demonstrations, weightsFailures with nothing broken: hazard analysis must cover losses where no part failed
Old network controls for a much bigger surface: GPU servers, vendor cloud, remote supportNew inputs for attackers: the scene and natural language (E1, E2)
 Rules still moving: EU dates in 2027 and 2028; in Japan, no specific rules yet for physical-AI machines
 Vendor safety claims to read closely: an inspection by the vendor's own lab is not a third-party certificate

First question for the client: "Which parts of your current robot-safety program still apply as they are, and which assume the robot only does what it was programmed to do?"

7. From PoC to production (PoCから本番展開までの組み込み方)

From Lesson 3 and Lesson 6.

Extension of conventional measuresNew for physical AI
Stage gates, release approvals, change managementRetraining as a change that needs re-validation, with no code change
Penetration tests, hardening, patching, incident responseStatistical evidence of learned behavior under written operating conditions
Risk assessment records, fences and conformity documents (Japan's 2013 routes, CE marking)Showing that a simulator is realistic before its results count as safety evidence
Japan's industrial-robot rules for a PoC on a production line (it is not R&D)Safety-case claims about the machine-learning part: operating context, unusual input, retraining
SBOM and vulnerability handling; safety cases for safety-critical systemsAn SBOM that also lists models, datasets and inference runtimes

First question for the client: "What evidence will you need at the end of the PoC, and is anyone collecting it now?"

How to use this with a client

  1. Start with the extensions. Show the client which parts of their current program already apply: information security (for example an ISMS), OT security (IEC 62443), machine safety (ISO 12100, ISO 10218, Japan's ordinance) and privacy. This builds trust and avoids starting from zero.
  2. Then show the new items as the gap. For each theme, the right column is a list of questions their program does not yet answer.
  3. Write findings in the template. The "extension or new" column of the assessment template's client summary uses the same idea.

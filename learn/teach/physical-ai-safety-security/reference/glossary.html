Glossary

The words this course uses, with one meaning each. A term joins this list when a lesson teaches it. "Avoid" lists words that mean the same thing or that blur the meaning. Use the main term instead.

Core ideas

Physical AI
AI that senses the physical world and acts in it through a machine, such as a robot arm.Avoid: embodied AI (common in research; this course uses one name).
VLA (vision-language-action model)
An AI model that takes camera images and a language instruction and outputs robot actions. In the running example, the VLA performs the grasp.
Doer / checker
A design in which a complex part (the doer) does the work and a simple, independent part (the checker) watches it and can always force a safe state.Also called in research: monitor/actuator pair, Simplex architecture, runtime assurance. Avoid: "AI safety layer" (says nothing about independence).
Safety function
A function that protects people, whose failure would increase the risk of harm. Example: a protective stop.
Safety-rated
Designed and certified to a functional-safety standard, such as ISO 13849-1 or IEC 62061, for a stated reliability level.Avoid: "safe", "safety-grade" (marketing words with no stated level).
Performance level (PL)
The ISO 13849-1 grade for how reliably a safety function works, written with a letter, for example "PL d".
Safe state
A condition in which the machine cannot cause harm. It is not always "power off": a legged robot falls when its power is cut.Avoid: "off".
Semantic safety
Avoiding harm that comes from the meaning of a task, such as which object goes where, rather than from the motion itself.
Trust boundary
In this course: the line between L3 and L4. Below it, no safety function depends on the AI or on the computer the AI runs on.
Reference monitor
A security mechanism that is always invoked, tamperproof, and small enough to verify (NIST). In this course, the model for a good checker.

The five layers (course model)

The numbers follow the path of a motion command, from the request down to the moving steel. They are not a ranking.

L1 Check the task
Checks before the AI acts: is the request or plan safe and clear? Refuses it, or asks a person.
L2 The AI itself
The VLA and the motion planner, trained and tested to do the job well. The untrusted doer.
L3 Check the motion
Software checks between the AI and the robot (work area, speed and joint limits, collisions, stale sensor data), with a simple fallback. Not certified.
L4 Certified safety functions
Safety-rated functions that work without the AI and its computer: protective stop, speed and separation monitoring, power and force limiting, safety-rated zones, emergency stop.
L5 Physical design and procedures
Guards, a layout without pinch points, low-energy tools, training, and work rules such as stopping and locking the robot before maintenance.

Safety functions (L4)

Protective stop
A stop that a safety function triggers to protect people, for example when a person comes too close.Avoid: calling it an emergency stop; that is the manual button.
Emergency stop (e-stop)
A stop that a person triggers by hand in an emergency, usually with a red button.
Speed and separation monitoring (SSM)
Safety-rated sensors measure the distance between a person and the robot; the robot slows down and stops before they can touch.
Power and force limiting (PFL)
The robot is designed so that any contact with a person stays below set force limits for each body region.

Japan

Motion range (可動範囲)
Under the labour ministry (MHLW) notice for industrial robots: the maximum range the robot's parts, tool included, can structurally move. Only electrical or mechanical stoppers make it smaller.

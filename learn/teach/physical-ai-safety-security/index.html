Course Started 15 October 2026 · Plain English · Eight lessons, 15–30 minutes each

Physical AI Safety & Security

A short course for a security engineer who advises on AI-driven robots. The running example is a robot arm doing pick-and-place, with classical motion planning for the approach and a VLA policy for the grasp.

Mission

Propose, review and test the safety and security of clients' physical AI projects, with the same confidence you have for secure software, and talk credibly with robotics safety experts.

Lessons

Lesson 1 · The AI Is Not the Safety System

Why an independent, certified checker must limit what an AI-driven robot can do, and how to tell which layer should stop which failure.

Lesson 2 · Threat-Model the Cell

Eight entry points with a real case for each, a data-flow diagram of the cell, how to trace an attack chain to physical harm, and which controls are extensions of conventional security and which are new.

Lesson 3 · Control the AI's Permissions and Motion Range

What the AI may ask the robot to do, where and how fast the arm may move, who may change each limit, and what ISO 10218:2025 and Japan's rules say about them.

Lesson 4 · Protect Data, Models and Know-How

How demonstrations, datasets and trained models carry a factory's know-how, five ways to lose them, what Japanese trade-secret law and contracts protect, and how to manage models, datasets and open-source parts (AIBOM, signing, retraining as a change).

Lesson 5 · Camera Data, Remote Operation and the Cloud

When camera images are personal information in Japan and the EU, what keeps remote operation safe when the link drops, and how to control vendor remote access and data sent to the cloud.

Lesson 6 · Build It In from PoC to Production

What to build in at each stage, what evidence each gate needs, how many trials a claim needs, and a minimal safety case.

Lesson 7 · Conventional Robots vs Physical AI

A side-by-side comparison for clients, and five questions that separate engineering evidence from marketing, with NVIDIA Halos as a worked case.

Lesson 8 · Capstone: Assess a Client Cell

A fictional client brief. Sort the findings, prioritize them by project stage, and write the top five for a steering committee.

Reference

Field guide

All the research on one page: seven findings, the five layers, a threat model, a map of standards and rules (international, EU, Japan), how to prove a system is safe, how to read vendor claims, and a checklist of questions for clients.

Assessment template

A structure for a real client assessment: scope, layers, entry points, data and models, video and remote access, rules, stage gates, vendor claims and a client summary.

Extension vs New

Every lesson's "extension or new" summary, organized by your seven themes, with a first question for the client on each.

Glossary

The course's words, with one meaning each. It grows with every lesson.

The map you will learn

Every lesson places itself on this map. A motion command passes through the layers from top to bottom. The dashed line is the trust boundary: below it, nothing depends on the AI or its computer.

Course path

All eight lessons are ready Ready. The course was re-planned on 16 October 2026 around the themes you need for client work. Each of Lessons 2 to 6 ends by sorting its controls into "extension of conventional measures" and "new for physical AI", and the Extension vs New page collects them by theme.

A suggested pace: two lessons a week, in order. Do the capstone last, a few days after Lesson 7, without looking back at the lessons first.

How to study

  • Answer the practice questions before you look anything up. Effortful recall is what builds memory.
  • After two or three days, try each lesson's "From memory" question again without looking. Each lesson's warm-up also asks about earlier lessons.
  • Ask Claude about anything that is unclear. Questions are part of the course.

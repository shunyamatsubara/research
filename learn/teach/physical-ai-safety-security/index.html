Physical AI Safety & Security

A short course for a security engineer who advises on AI-driven robots. The running example is a robot arm doing pick-and-place, with classical motion planning for the approach and a VLA policy for the grasp.

Mission

Propose, review and test the safety and security of clients' physical AI projects, with the same confidence you have for secure software, and talk credibly with robotics safety experts.

Lessons

Lesson 1 · The AI Is Not the Safety System

Why an independent, certified checker must limit what an AI-driven robot can do, and how to tell which layer should stop which failure.

Lesson 2 · Threat-Model the Cell

Eight entry points with a real case for each, a data-flow diagram of the cell, how to trace an attack chain to physical harm, and which controls are extensions of conventional security and which are new.

Reference

Field guide

All the research on one page: seven findings, the five layers, a threat model, a map of standards and rules (international, EU, Japan), how to prove a system is safe, how to read vendor claims, and a checklist of questions for clients.

Glossary

The course's words, with one meaning each. It grows with every lesson.

The map you will learn

Every lesson places itself on this map. A motion command passes through the layers from top to bottom. The dashed line is the trust boundary: below it, nothing depends on the AI or its computer.

Course path

Re-planned on 16 October 2026 around the themes you need for client work. Each lesson ends by sorting its controls into "extension of conventional measures" and "new for physical AI".

  1. The AI is not the safety system Ready
  2. Threat-model the cell: eight entry points, attack chains, and extension versus new. Ready
  3. Control the AI's permissions and motion range: what the AI may command, who may change the limits, and how the standards and Japan's rules set them. Next
  4. Protect data, models and know-how, and manage AI and OSS components: confidentiality of training data and know-how, model and dataset versions, SBOM, retraining as a change.
  5. Camera data, remote operation and the cloud: privacy and confidentiality of video, remote operation and what happens when the link drops.
  6. From PoC to production: what to build in at each stage, test statistics, and a minimal safety case.
  7. Conventional robots versus physical AI, and reading vendor claims: a side-by-side comparison, with NVIDIA Halos as a case study.
  8. Capstone: a short assessment of a sample client cell.

How to study

  • Answer the practice questions before you look anything up. Effortful recall is what builds memory.
  • After two or three days, try each lesson's "From memory" question again without looking.
  • Ask Claude about anything that is unclear. Questions are part of the course.

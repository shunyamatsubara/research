# Physical-AI cybersecurity: research brief (as of 15 Oct 2026)

**How this was done.** I read the primary sources in full wherever possible. That includes both starting articles (and the USCSI threat table, which exists only as an image), the ROS 2 threat model, the Alias Robotics Unitree G1 report and brief, the UniPwn disclosure, the official CVE JSON records, the EU legal texts from the Publications Office, the CISA AI-in-OT PDF and the MITRE ATLAS v2026.09 data. The shared web-search quota ran out partway through, so the later checks used only direct fetches of known primary URLs. Anything I could not confirm is marked **unverified**.

## 1. Key findings

### 1a. The two starting sources

**Exein, "Threat modeling physical AI: when the attack surface is the world"** (Thomas Roos, 10 Jul 2026), https://www.exein.io/blog/threat-modeling-physical-ai. *Vendor marketing, but technically substantive.*
- **Threat-model structure.** It names three shifts that break classic embedded threat models:
  - every sensor becomes an instruction channel ("Anyone who can put a sign in front of a camera can put text into the model's context");
  - behaviour is probabilistic, so there is no patch;
  - failures act physically, with nobody watching.

  It then says to "model the loop, not the components." Perception is the injection surface, reasoning is the hijack surface, action is where harm happens, memory/state lets an attack persist (including tampered weights), and connectivity spreads it across the fleet. Its asset list: perceived data, actuators, credentials and network position, model weights and outputs, and the fleet.
- **Recommended controls.**
  - Assume the model is compromised and enforce rules below it, in the kernel, OS or hardware, with default-deny allowlists.
  - Give the agent its own non-root identity with the minimum permissions.
  - Put weights, inference runtime and agent framework in the SBOM and scan them before shipping.
  - Watch the enforcement boundary across the fleet.
  - Reuse the threat model as the AI part of the CRA risk assessment.

  Key line: "Any mitigation that depends on the model behaving is not a mitigation."
- **Verdict: substantive but narrow.** It is a good mental model for edge devices where an LLM or VLM calls tools, and it matches Microsoft's 26 Feb 2026 guidance (https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/security/blog/2026/02/26/threat-modeling-ai-applications/, *vendor technical documentation*). Its gaps:
  - no incidents or data;
  - "below the model" means the OS kernel, not a safety-rated motion controller;
  - nothing on functional safety, ROS 2/DDS, real-time control, or VLA policies that output continuous joint actions;
  - it plugs two Exein products.

**USCSI, "Physical AI Security: Key Risks and How to Address Them"** (byline "Admin", 6 Oct 2026), https://www.uscsinstitute.org/cybersecurity-insights/blog/physical-ai-security-key-risks-and-how-to-address-them. *Vendor marketing; the publisher sells a certification.*
- **Seven threats (image only):** sensor spoofing, ROS 2 middleware attacks, wireless/network fleet compromise, physical prompt injection, supply chain, hardware tampering, unauthorized physical actuation.
- **Six mitigations:** patching plus SROS2, redundant sensor validation, fleet segmentation, supply-chain provenance, physical access control, behavioural monitoring.
- **Verdict: generic.** It cites no primary sources. Its only statistic (Capgemini: 1,678 executives, 67% call physical AI a "game-changer") is unlinked and **unverified**. It has no incidents, no layered design, and ends by selling a certification. Its claim that SROS2 "resolves" middleware weakness overstates things: peer-reviewed work found SROS2 design flaws (see §3). Use it only as a vocabulary checklist.

### 1b. Q1: What a good physical-AI threat model looks like
- **Use the ROS 2 Robotic Systems Threat Model as the skeleton** (written Mar 2019, last modified Jan 2021): https://design.ros2.org/articles/ros2_threat_model.html, *vendor technical documentation (open-source project)*. It uses STRIDE for threat categories and DREAD for scoring, and includes a 6-DoF arm case study (MARA).
  - **Assets:** sensor data and logs; "physical safety"; actuator-command and behaviour integrity; availability.
  - **Entry points:** DDS/ROS topics, SSH/VNC, cloud and phone apps, code deployment, sensors, USB/debug ports and internal buses, Wi-Fi/cellular/Bluetooth.
  - **Actors:** robot user, developer, third-party robot, teleoperator, cloud developer, cloud service.
  - It states its own limits: "Mitigating all attacks in this document does not ensure any robotic product is secure."
- **Add the AI layer.** Trace attack chains across stages, as Exein recommends. Treat the scene itself as an input channel: MITRE ATLAS AML.T0041 says "If the model is interacting with data collected from the real world in some way, the adversary can influence the model" (https://atlas.mitre.org/techniques/AML.T0041, *independent expert*). The Sep 2026 ATLAS release adds AML.T0129, "Triggers in Multimodal Inputs" (https://github.com/mitre-atlas/atlas-data).
- **Trust boundaries for this learner's arm** (planner handles the approach, VLA handles the grasp). Each has a documented precedent in §3:
  - camera → VLA (adversarial patches, signs, backdoor triggers)
  - operator language → planner/VLA (jailbreaks)
  - VLA/planner → controller (unbounded commands)
  - the network link to the policy server
  - model and dataset provenance
  - the ROS 2/DDS graph
  - BLE/Wi-Fi setup (provisioning)
  - cloud, fleet, OTA updates and telemetry
  - vendor remote-access tunnels
  - physical and debug-port access

### 1c. Q2: Incidents (details in §3)
Documented *malicious* attacks on robots are rare. A 2025 survey states "no major, publicly documented cyberattacks have targeted humanoids to date" (quoted in https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.14096). The evidence is mostly product vulnerabilities (Unitree, DDS, LeRobot) and peer-reviewed attack demonstrations. Consumer-robot fleet abuse (Ecovacs 2024, DJI 2026) is the closest thing to real-world incidents.

### 1d. Q3: Controls layer by layer (★ = specific to physical AI)
1. ★ **A safety layer that a compromised AI cannot override.**
   - The ROS 2 threat model: "Risk is mitigated when applying limits on actuator drivers."
   - EU Machinery Regulation, Annex III 1.2.1: control systems must withstand "reasonably foreseeable malicious attempts from third parties leading to a hazardous situation". Self-evolving logic "shall not cause the machinery or related product to perform actions beyond its defined task and movement space" (http://data.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/1230/oj, *regulator*).
   - CISA and partners: "AI such as LLMs almost certainly should not be used to make safety decisions for OT environments." They also say: "Ensure there are failsafe mechanisms that revert to traditional automation or manual for any AI-enabled system processes" (3 Dec 2025, https://www.cisa.gov/resources-tools/resources/principles-secure-integration-artificial-intelligence-operational-technology, *regulator*).
   - **Implication:** speed, force and workspace limits and stop functions belong in a safety-rated controller below both the VLA and the planner. Keeping the VLA out of safety functions also avoids the Machinery Regulation's third-party conformity assessment, which Annex I requires for "safety components with fully or partially self-evolving behaviour using machine learning approaches".
2. ★ **Check every action the AI issues, outside the AI.** OWASP LLM06: "Implement authorization in downstream systems rather than relying on an LLM to decide if an action is allowed or not" (https://genai.owasp.org/llmrisk/llm062025-excessive-agency/, *independent expert*). RoboGuard (IEEE RA-L, accepted 16 Feb 2026) "reduces the execution of unsafe plans from over 92% to below 3%" under jailbreaking attacks (https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.07885, *peer-reviewed*).
3. ★ **Perception.** Detect noisy, out-of-range or disconnected sensors (ROS 2 threat model) and treat any text in the camera's view as hostile. The CHAI authors say defenses must "extend beyond traditional adversarial robustness" (https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.00181, *peer-reviewed*).
4. **Model and data supply chain.**
   - Never unpickle untrusted files. Hugging Face says its pickle scanning "is not 100% foolproof" (https://huggingface.co/docs/hub/security-pickle, *vendor technical documentation*).
   - PyTorch before 2.6.0 allowed remote code execution even with `weights_only=True` (https://www.cve.org/CVERecord?id=CVE-2025-32434, *CVE record*).
   - Sign and verify model files (https://github.com/sigstore/model-transparency, *vendor technical documentation*). Put weights and runtimes in the SBOM.
   - ★ Backdoors survive fine-tuning (BadVLA), so control who trains or fine-tunes the policy and on which demonstrations.
5. **Policy serving.** LeRobot's async inference used `pickle.loads()` on data "received over unauthenticated gRPC channels without TLS" (https://www.cve.org/CVERecord?id=CVE-2026-25874). Use mutual TLS, safetensors/JSON instead of pickle, and keep the link inside the robot cell's network.
6. **ROS 2 / DDS.**
   - Security is off by default: "Security has been turned off by default, so security features will not be enabled even when the proper security files are present." Badly configured participants still launch "without security features" unless `ROS_SECURITY_STRATEGY=Enforce` is set (https://docs.ros.org/en/kilted/Concepts/Intermediate/About-Security.html, *vendor technical documentation*; read via its GitHub source).
   - Discovery default: "By default, ROS 2 will attempt to find all nodes on all hosts on the same subnet automatically" (https://docs.ros.org/en/kilted/Tutorials/Advanced/Improved-Dynamic-Discovery.html).
   - Harden the governance file (reject unauthenticated participants, protect discovery) and limit actuator topics to the nodes that need them (ROS 2 threat model). Patch the DDS implementation.
7. **Host and firmware.** Verified boot, keys held in a TPM, and disk encryption (ROS 2 threat model). Run agents as non-root with default-deny policies (Exein). Use unique keys per device and escape every provisioning input.
8. **Cloud, fleet and OTA.** Check per-device *authorization*, not just login: "authentication was present, but authorization was missing" (https://boschko.ca/g1-ble-rce/, *independent expert*). Use signed, encrypted updates with certificate pinning. Allowlist outbound traffic and inventory vendor telemetry and remote-access services.
9. **Network.** Use IEC 62443 zones and conduits. Prefer push-only or one-way data flows so the AI system "is not a persistent attack path into OT" (CISA, above).
10. **Evidence and reporting.**
    - Machinery Regulation 1.1.9: collect evidence of any "legitimate or illegitimate intervention". 1.2.1(f): keep tracing logs of safety-software versions for five years. Self-evolving safety systems must keep safety decision data for one year.
    - Cyber Resilience Act (CRA): 24 h early warning and 72 h notification for actively exploited vulnerabilities.

### 1e. Q4: What maps to a security engineer's existing knowledge, and what is new
- **Maps directly:**
  - STRIDE (the ROS 2 threat model uses STRIDE with DREAD scoring)
  - defense in depth
  - least privilege (per-node SROS2 permissions; OWASP LLM06)
  - SBOM (required by CRA Annex I)
  - secure boot
  - coordinated vulnerability disclosure
  - input validation (UniPwn is plain CWE-78 command injection)
  - unsafe deserialization (LeRobot is CWE-502)
  - segmentation (IEC 62443 zones/conduits)
- **Genuinely new:**
  1. The scene itself is an attack channel that sends no network packets (CHAI, adversarial patches).
  2. Backdoors in learned policies are invisible to code review (BadVLA, TrojanRobot).
  3. Steerability is probabilistic and cannot be patched (Exein, Microsoft).
  4. The impact is kinetic, so security must be co-engineered with functional safety (IEC TR 63069, IEC TS 63074). Safety mechanisms can themselves become attack tools: a single packet could trigger the Raven II surgical robot's E-stop.
  5. One secret shared across a whole fleet, plus radio range, makes robots wormable (UniPwn).
  6. Machinery law adds its own duties: tamper evidence, log retention, and third-party assessment of ML safety components.
- Exein argues that STRIDE applied component by component misses multi-step agent attacks. Apply STRIDE per component, and also trace chains across the loop.

### 1f. Regulation and standards status (checked October 2026)
- **EU Machinery Regulation 2023/1230.** "Applies on a mandatory basis as of 20 January 2027". The original Official Journal text said 14 January; a corrigendum fixed this (https://single-market-economy.ec.europa.eu/sectors/mechanical-engineering/machinery_en, *regulator*). Annex III 1.1.9: safety-critical software and data "shall be adequately protected against accidental or intentional corruption".
- **Digital Omnibus on AI, Reg. (EU) 2026/1744.** Published 24 Jul 2026, in force 27 Jul 2026 (http://data.europa.eu/eli/reg/2026/1744/oj, *regulator*).
  - It moves machinery to Section B of AI Act Annex I.
  - The Commission must add AI requirements to Machinery Regulation Annex III by delegated acts: "Those delegated acts shall apply by 2 August 2028."
  - AI Act high-risk obligations now apply from 2 Dec 2027 (Annex III systems) and 2 Aug 2028 (Annex I products).
  - AI Act Art. 15(5) covers attacks that "manipulate the training data set (data poisoning), or pre-trained components used in training (model poisoning)", plus adversarial examples (http://data.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj).
- **Cyber Resilience Act (Reg. 2024/2847).**
  - "Article 14 shall apply from 11 September 2026 and Chapter IV (Articles 35 to 51) shall apply from 11 June 2026"; the rest applies from 11 Dec 2027 (http://data.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/2847/oj).
  - ENISA's Single Reporting Platform has been "operational as of 11 September 2026" (https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/cra-reporting).
  - Annex I requires secure-by-default configuration, integrity of "commands, programs and configuration", and an SBOM.
- **ISO 10218-1/-2:2025** (published Feb 2025) add cybersecurity clauses for the first time, according to a Feb 2026 preprint (https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17822, *preprint*):
  - Part 1 §5.1.16: cyber threat assessment, secured interfaces, authentication, encryption where needed.
  - Part 2 §5.2.16 and §7.5.23: integrator duties.
  - Per the same preprint, the 2025 edition makes ISO/TS 15066 (collaborative robots) normative.
  - I did not read the standard itself (paywalled).
- **IEC standards on safety and security.** IEC TS 63074:2023 applies IEC 62443 to the safety-related control systems of machinery (stability date 2027; https://webstore.iec.ch/en/publication/69228, *standards body*). IEC TR 63069:2019 covers using IEC 61508 and IEC 62443 together; its framing question is "How to design and manage safety and security – in cooperation, integrated, or separate system?" (https://webstore.iec.ch/en/publication/31421).
- **Other status checks:**
  - OMG DDS Security 1.2 was published Feb 2026 (https://www.omg.org/spec/DDS-SECURITY/).
  - MITRE ATLAS content release v2026.09 (14 Sep 2026): 16 tactics and 120 techniques. My keyword check of its 73 case studies found none involving robots.
  - The OWASP LLM Top 10 2025 is still the current edition (https://genai.owasp.org/llm-top-10/).
  - The OWASP Top 10 for Agentic Applications 2026 was published 9 Dec 2025 (https://genai.owasp.org/resource/owasp-top-10-for-agentic-applications-for-2026/).
  - CISA Secure by Design (Apr 2023, revised 25 Oct 2023) has three principles, starting with "Take Ownership of Customer Security Outcomes" (https://www.cisa.gov/resources-tools/resources/secure-by-design).

## 2. Concepts and terms
- **Physical AI**: a model running on a device that perceives through sensors and acts through actuators. Source: Exein (URL above).
- **VLA (vision-language-action) model**: a model that maps camera images and a language instruction directly to robot actions. https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.13587
- **Robot jailbreak**: prompts that make an LLM-controlled robot perform actions its guardrails should refuse. https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.13691
- **Physical (environmental) prompt injection**: instructions planted in the scene, such as a sign, that the model obeys. https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.00181
- **Adversarial patch**: a small printed pattern in the camera's view that derails the model's output. https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.13587
- **Backdoor / trigger**: hidden behaviour trained into a model that activates only when a specific trigger appears. https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.16640
- **Unsafe AI artifact**: a model file whose loading runs code or changes behaviour, for example a pickle. https://atlas.mitre.org/techniques/AML.T0011.000
- **safetensors**: a weights-only file format that avoids pickle's code execution. https://huggingface.co/docs/hub/security-pickle
- **DDS Security**: the OMG specification for authentication, access-control, cryptographic, logging and data-tagging plugins. https://www.omg.org/spec/DDS-SECURITY/
- **SROS2 enclave**: the certificates plus signed governance and permissions files that define what a group of ROS 2 nodes may do. ROS docs (URL above).
- **Zone / conduit**: a group of assets "that share common security requirements" / the channels connecting zones. https://dragos.com/blog/isa-iec-62443-concepts (*vendor technical documentation*, 8 Jan 2025)
- **Protection against corruption**: the Machinery Regulation duty that connections, software and data cannot be corrupted into a hazard, and that interventions leave evidence. Machinery Regulation URL above.
- **Self-evolving behaviour**: machinery whose logic changes through learning; it must stay within its defined task and movement space. Machinery Regulation URL above.
- **Excessive agency**: when an LLM-based system "enables damaging actions to be performed in response to unexpected, ambiguous or manipulated outputs" from the model. OWASP LLM06 URL above.
- **Actively exploited vulnerability**: one with "reliable evidence that a malicious actor has exploited it"; this triggers CRA reporting. CRA URL above.
- **Safety–security co-engineering**: designing functional safety (IEC 61508) and security (IEC 62443) together. IEC TR 63069 URL above.
- **Wormable**: "An infected robot can simply scan for other Unitree robots in BLE range and automatically compromise them". https://github.com/Bin4ry/UniPwn

## 3. Incidents, attacks, numbers, dates
- **Apr 2015 — Raven II teleoperated surgical robot.** Researchers could override surgeon commands, and "it is possible to abuse the robot's existing emergency stop (E-stop) mechanism to execute efficient (single packet) attacks". https://arxiv.org/abs/1504.04339 (*preprint*)
- **May 2017 — Industrial robot controller (Trend Micro / Politecnico di Milano).** Five attack classes on an ABB industrial controller: control-loop parameters, calibration, production logic, the robot state the operator sees, and the true robot state. Exposure counts as of late Mar 2017: 28 robots and 83,673 industrial routers internet-exposed, 5,105 of the routers with no authentication. "By exploiting security issues, an attacker may disrupt operations and indirectly pose safety threats to human operators." Sources: https://documents.trendmicro.com/assets/wp/wp-industrial-robot-security.pdf (*vendor technical documentation*) and https://doi.org/10.1109/SP.2017.20 (*peer-reviewed*, IEEE S&P 2017).
- **Jul 2018 — Internet scan for ROS.** Found "over 100 publicly-accessible hosts that are running a ROS master node"; with consent, the researchers moved a university robot. https://arxiv.org/abs/1808.03322 (*preprint*)
- **Nov 2019 — LiDAR spoofing against autonomous-vehicle perception.** Attack success around 75%. https://doi.org/10.1145/3319535.3339815 (*peer-reviewed*, CCS)
- **11 Nov 2021 — CISA ICSA-21-315-02.** 13 CVEs across six DDS implementations (CycloneDDS, Fast DDS, GurumDDS, OpenDDS, RTI Connext, CoreDX); highest CVSS 8.6. https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/ics-advisories/icsa-21-315-02 (*regulator*)
- **Nov 2022 — Four SROS2 design flaws.** They let an adversary "totally invalidate the security protection offered by SROS2"; the authors say the fix was adopted upstream. https://doi.org/10.1145/3548606.3560681 (*peer-reviewed*, CCS)
- **May–Oct 2024 — Ecovacs Deebot X2 hijacks.** Robots in several US cities had their speakers abused and one chased a dog. Researchers had reported a PIN-bypass flaw in Dec 2023; the root cause is disputed. https://www.engadget.com/cybersecurity/hackers-are-making-robot-vacuums-randomly-yell-racial-slurs-184017187.html (*press*, 11 Oct 2024)
- **17 Oct 2024 — RoboPAIR.** Jailbreaks "often achieving 100% attack success rates". The Unitree Go2 result was "the first successful jailbreak of a deployed commercial robotic system". https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.13691 (*peer-reviewed*: ICRA 2025, DOI 10.1109/ICRA55743.2025.11128119)
- **Nov 2024 — Adversarial patches against VLAs.** Task success dropped "up to a 100% reduction" in simulation; a physical patch also worked. https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.13587 (ICCV 2025 according to the arXiv note)
- **Nov 2024 (revised Apr 2026) — TrojanRobot.** Supply-chain backdoor in VLM-based manipulation, tested on 18 real-world tasks. https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.11683 (*preprint*)
- **27–28 Mar 2025 — CVE-2025-2894, Unitree Go1.** The robot "contains an undocumented backdoor" (CloudSail remote-access tunnel); CWE-912, CVSS 6.6. A known API key has since been revoked. https://www.cve.org/CVERecord?id=CVE-2025-2894 and https://takeonme.org/cves/cve-2025-2894/ (*CVE record*, *independent expert*)
- **May 2025 — BadVLA.** "BadVLA consistently achieves near-100% attack success rates with minimal impact on clean task accuracy." https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/b94925a92f2271cd60c9f3f7a7d366fe-Abstract-Conference.html (*peer-reviewed*, NeurIPS 2025)
- **9–17 Sep 2025 — Alias Robotics assessment of the Unitree G1 humanoid.**
  - Telemetry sent "to 43.175.228.18:17883 and 43.175.229.18:17883 every 300 seconds without operator notice", at about 1.03 and 0.39 Mbps.
  - One encryption key used across the whole fleet: "compromising one device exposes the entire fleet".
  - DDS traffic is "unencrypted and susceptible to local interception"; the WebRTC client has TLS verification disabled; it runs ROS 2 Foxy, which reached end of life in May 2023.
  - Caveat: the authors' "limited telemetry inspection didn't allow us to confirm" that audio and video are in what gets uploaded.
  - Sources: https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.14139 and https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.14096 (*preprint* by a security vendor; disclosed to Unitree before publication).
- **20–26 Sep 2025 — UniPwn (Unitree Go2, G1, H1, B2).**
  - Flaws: a hard-coded BLE key, an authentication check that accepts the string "unitree", and command execution "as root via the wpa_supplicant_restart.sh shell script". The flaw is wormable.
  - CVEs: CVE-2025-35027 (CVSS 7.3), CVE-2025-60017 (8.2), CVE-2025-60250 and CVE-2025-60251.
  - Unitree on 29 Sep 2025: "have now completed the majority of the fixes".
  - Sources: https://github.com/Bin4ry/UniPwn, https://www.cve.org/CVERecord?id=CVE-2025-35027, https://spectrum.ieee.org/unitree-robot-exploit (*press*).
- **30 Sep 2025 — CHAI (prompt injection via signs in the scene).** Up to 95.5% attack success in simulation and "up to ≥ 87% ASR" on a real robotic vehicle. https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.00181 (*peer-reviewed*, IEEE SaTML 2026)
- **Feb 2026 — DJI Romo robot vacuums.** DJI's MQTT broker had "no topic-level access controls", so about 7,000 robots in 24 countries answered one user's credentials, exposing cameras, microphone audio and floor plans. Patches shipped 8–10 Feb; one issue was still open on 17 Feb. https://dronexl.co/2026/02/17/dji-romo-security-breach/ (*press*)
- **23 Apr 2026 — CVE-2026-25874, LeRobot ≤0.5.1.** CVSS v4 score 9.3; CISA rates it as having a proof of concept and being automatable. On 7 Oct 2026 the fix PR was closed without merging because the async inference module is being replaced. I found no release that contains a fix. https://www.cve.org/CVERecord?id=CVE-2026-25874 and https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/pull/3048
- **27 Aug 2026 — CVE-2026-76639 (CVSS 3.1 score 8.8) and CVE-2026-76640 (7.5), Unitree G1 EDU ≤1.5.2.**
  - Root access via "an unauthenticated WebRTC-to-DDS bridge on TCP port 9991" combined with a path-traversal flaw, and separately via a BLE buffer overflow.
  - The researcher found the CycloneDDS security plugins in the build but not enabled.
  - Unitree paid $5,000 in bug bounties. Firmware 1.5.5.0 (1 Sep 2026) "reportedly blocks" the exploit chain.
  - Sources: https://boschko.ca/g1-ble-rce/, https://www.cve.org/CVERecord?id=CVE-2026-76639

Inside Volvo's Driving Stack Reference Lesson 1

Reference · checked 16 Oct 2026

Stack map

Who owns which job in Volvo's future driving system, the numbers worth remembering, and the names that NVIDIA keeps changing.

The four owners

PartWhat it isJobs it ownsRobot comparison
Hyperion NVIDIA's tested hardware design: “sensor and compute architecture.”1 The sensor set; the two DRIVE AGX Thor computers; the board design.3 The robot's sensor rig and onboard computer, bought as a validated kit.
Halos OS The in-car part of NVIDIA Halos, the safety system. Certified base software.4 Walled-off safety core (hypervisor); classical perception and planning; rule-based guardrails for the AI.45 Real-time OS plus a separate safety controller and a classical planner that the policy cannot override.
Alpamayo NVIDIA's reasoning VLA for driving. Big open models are teachers; a compact model runs in the car.67 Turn camera video into a planned path, plus a written reason (“Chain-of-Causation”).58 The VLA grasp policy, trained large in the cloud and distilled for the robot's GPU.
Volvo The carmaker. The car itself, integration, and its own “vehicle safety systems and engineering expertise.”1 You, the system integrator who owns the final product.

The pattern: two stacks at the same time

  • “The point is we run these two stacks at the same time.” (Ali Kani, NVIDIA)2
  • An AI end-to-end stack for core driving runs “alongside a parallel classical safety stack” built on Halos.9
  • “Policy Evaluator and Safety Monitors” evaluate driving actions and “support trajectory selection.”5 How the final path is chosen is not public.
  • Your robot design splits by phase (planner, then VLA). This stack splits by role (both run, a checker decides).

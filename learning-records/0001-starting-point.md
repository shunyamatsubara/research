# Starting point: knows MECE, logic trees and common biases

At the first session (2026-10-16) the user said they "know a few" thinking frameworks, such as MECE, logic trees and common cognitive biases, but have not studied the broader critical-thinking frameworks (Paul–Elder, Pólya, issue-driven thinking) in depth. Their pain points are too much input, hidden assumptions found late, and analysis without a clear "so what". They did not pick "fuzzy question" as a pain point. Their main uses are learning new fields and writing/arguing.

**Evidence**: Self-reported in the first-session interview.

**Implications**: Don't re-teach MECE or basic bias lists; use them as hooks. Lesson 1 maps the three pain points onto links of a chain of thinking. Watch for a likely blind spot: "too much input" is usually a question problem (no sharp question means everything looks relevant), even though the user did not pick "fuzzy question".
